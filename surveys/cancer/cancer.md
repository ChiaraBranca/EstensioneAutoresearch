# Living Survey: cancer

## Introduction

This document collects the scientific literature regarding **cancer**.

## Spatial Transcriptomics and Pan-Cancer Metastasis
Recent advances in spatially resolved single-cell transcriptomics have provided unprecedented insights into the tumor microenvironment (TME) and metastatic processes. Furthermore, pan-cancer co-expression network analyses have identified hub genes that not only define tumor states but also show strong potential for predicting patient responses to targeted therapies [^10.5281_zenodo.2574643].

## Metabolic Reprogramming and Nutritional Interventions
Cancer cells exhibit profound metabolic plasticity, which can be exploited through dietary and nutritional strategies. Omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids, particularly eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA), have been shown to induce mitochondrial succinylation, disrupting metabolic homeostasis and triggering ferroptosis in prostate cancer cells [^10.17638_03195029]. Similarly, cyclic fasting and fasting-mimicking diets elevate systemic polyunsaturated fatty acids, which cooperate with nutrient starvation to induce lipid peroxidation and ferroptosis, demonstrating significant antitumor efficacy in breast cancer models [^10.21954_ou.ro.00108036].

## Oncogenic Signaling and Chromatin Regulation
Dysregulation of key signaling pathways and chromatin architecture remains a central driver of tumorigenesis. Deubiquitinating enzymes (DUBs) have emerged as critical regulators of the Hippo pathway, with USP42 identified as a key modulator of YAP/TAZ activity in pleural mesothelioma [^10.17638_03193849]. Concurrently, the DEK oncoprotein has been characterized as a dynamic chromatin architectural factor that drives transcriptional reprogramming and metastatic progression in melanoma, with its activity heavily dependent on CK2-mediated phosphorylation [^10.17638_03194520].

## Liquid Biopsy and Non-Invasive Diagnostics
The shift towards non-invasive diagnostics has accelerated the development of liquid biopsy techniques. Cell-free DNA (cfDNA) profiling, particularly focusing on nucleosome positioning and repetitive genomic elements, has proven highly effective in distinguishing cancer patients from healthy controls across multiple tumor types, including glioblastoma and colorectal cancer [^10.5526_err-00042862][^10.5526_err-00042900]. Additionally, comprehensive RNA-seq analyses in specific cohorts have mapped differential gene expression landscapes in colorectal cancer, highlighting cell cycle and IL-17 pathways as potential therapeutic targets [^10.5281_zenodo.8307739]. Urinary proteomics is also being leveraged to identify novel biomarkers for early breast cancer detection [^10.5281_zenodo.20393677].

## Multi-Omics Prognostics and Therapeutic Development
Integrating multi-omic data is revolutionizing prognostic modeling and drug discovery. Computational frameworks analyzing over 10,000 checkpoint blockade treatment cases have successfully dissected tumor-immune ecosystems, identifying prognostic cellular programs across diverse malignancies [^10.5281_zenodo.18706180][^10.5281_zenodo.18706179].
