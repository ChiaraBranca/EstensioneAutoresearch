# Living Survey: cancer

## Introduction

This document collects the scientific literature regarding **cancer**.

## Computational Diagnostics & Imaging

Recent advances in medical imaging and spatial transcriptomics are significantly enhancing early detection and tissue characterization. In parallel, microbiome-based detection frameworks like BreCol provide rigorous benchmarks across multiple cancer types, highlighting the persistent challenge of generalizing classical and deep-learning models across temporal and cohort boundaries [^2609.27207]. Foundation models are also being adapted for mammography; embeddings from domain-specific and general medical imaging models can capture longitudinal pre-diagnostic tissue changes, with clinically grounded pretraining proving essential for detecting subtle malignant drifts [^2609.26605]. To address post-treatment monitoring, Mammo-LIFE introduces a multimodal framework that fuses longitudinal mammographic features with clinical variables via low-rank adaptation and view-attention pooling to predict radiotherapy outcomes [^2609.26443]. Beyond imaging, non-invasive diagnostic modalities are expanding: terahertz spectroscopy of urine vapors shows promise in distinguishing prostate cancer from benign conditions [^2609.23814], while whole-slide pathology foundation models like WILSON enable patient-level multi-magnification analysis that surpasses dedicated case-level models in diagnostic text retrieval and generation [^2609.25123]. Explainability remains critical for clinical adoption; formal concept analysis-guided counterfactual explanations have demonstrated perfect validity and emergent sparsity for multi-modal breast cancer diagnosis, offering actionable insights where traditional attribution methods fail [^2609.20067]. Furthermore, standardized datasets like ERCPMP-Gx are bridging the gap between endoscopic phenotypes, histopathology, and germline genetics to support AI-driven classification of hereditary polyposis syndromes [^2609.20815]. Liquid biopsy techniques are also maturing, with cell-free DNA nucleosome positioning profiling establishing robust, repeat-based signatures that discriminate healthy individuals from patients across multiple cancer types [^10.5526_err-00042900]. Complementary proteomic approaches, such as urinary proteomics in breast cancer cohorts, continue to identify novel biomarker candidates for non-invasive screening [^10.5281_zenodo.20393677].

## Tumor Evolution & Microenvironment

Understanding the physical and evolutionary dynamics of tumor progression is crucial for predicting invasion and metastasis. Research into intratumor heterogeneity reveals that the order of driver mutation acquisition fundamentally shapes clonal structure; threshold-like fitness effects and order-dependent terminal fitness can drive highly concentrated malignant populations or cause faster-growing pathways to dominate asymptotically [^2609.25838]. The tumor microenvironment also plays a mechanical role in progression: tumor-induced lipid loss in adipocytes physically remodels white adipose tissue, accelerating breast cancer invasion under conditions of elevated tissue pressure, a phenomenon exacerbated by obesity [^2609.23229]. Mechanistic modeling of tumor growth is advancing through 2D reaction-diffusion frameworks that estimate cell diffusivity and proliferation rates from single-point biopsies, providing theoretical validation for dispersion length and front velocity as risk-stratification biomarkers [^2609.22363]. At the metastatic front, single-cell and spatial multi-omics are uncovering confined tumor microenvironments in clear cell renal cell carcinoma lung metastases, characterized by cancer-associated fibroblast lymphocyte exclusion sites [^10.5281_zenodo.18767278]. Similarly, pan-cancer brain metastasis atlases are being constructed to map spatially resolved cellular states across diverse primary origins [^10.5281_zenodo.21816284]. Oncogenic chromatin remodeling further drives metastasis; the DEK oncoprotein functions as a context-dependent transcriptional regulator in melanoma, with its binding landscape and phosphorylation status directly influencing chromatin compaction and tumor progression [^10.17638_03194520].

## Therapeutic Optimization & Immunotherapy

Translating computational insights into clinical treatment strategies requires robust frameworks for personalized therapy and toxicity management. Federated learning architectures are being deployed to optimize pancreatic cancer treatment across distributed sites, overcoming data privacy barriers while handling partially overlapping local features [^2609.24718]. Bridging the gap between trial data and real-world practice, transportability methods combine routine care data with trial summary measures to estimate absolute treatment effects, as demonstrated in breast cancer endocrine therapy outcomes [^2609.24305]. In radiation oncology, uncertainty-quantified ensemble models predict severe radiotherapy-induced immunosuppression in esophageal cancer, enabling risk-aware proton therapy selection and dose optimization [^2609.25139]. Quantum-classical hybrid attention mechanisms are being explored for histopathology-based molecular profiling, showing selective gains in data-limited cancer cohorts by redistributing predictive accuracy toward prognostically relevant biological targets [^2609.21115]. Immunotherapy development benefits from hybrid quantum-classical neural networks that improve sample efficiency in peptide-HLA binding prediction, a critical step for neoantigen identification in personalized cancer vaccines [^2609.19642]. Metabolic interventions are also yielding novel therapeutic avenues; omega-3 fatty acid interventions induce mitochondrial succinylation networks that disrupt homeostasis and trigger ferroptosis in prostate cancer cells [^10.17638_03195029]. Signaling pathway modulation remains a key target, with deubiquitinating enzymes like USP42 identified as critical regulators of YAP/TAZ activity in mesothelioma, offering potential therapeutic entry points [^10.17638_03193849]. Nutritional strategies are being re-evaluated through lipidomics, revealing that polyunsaturated fatty acids mediate the antitumor effects of fasting by inducing lipid peroxidation and ferroptosis in triple-negative breast cancer [^10.21954_ou.ro.00108036]. Finally, targeted small-molecule therapies continue to evolve, with non-genotoxic topoisomerase II catalytic inhibitors being characterized for their potential to bypass conventional resistance mechanisms [^10.14288_1.0451818].

## Multi-Omics & Molecular Profiling

Non-parametric estimators for excess recurrent events are being adapted to cancer cohorts, combining patient-level data with population statistics to quantify disease burden in rectal cancer [^2609.26328]. Variable selection in ultra-high-dimensional genomic data is improved by structured screen-and-select frameworks that leverage predictor correlations [^2609.24945]. At the proteome level, ridge regression-based feature selection identifies stable transcriptomic predictors for thousands of proteins across cancer cell lines, revealing globally predictive and context-specific gene modules [^2609.24004]. Bayesian inference for mixed graphical models is being applied to heterogeneous data, successfully recovering meaningful gene expression dependencies and subtype relationships in breast cancer datasets [^2609.23857]. Regulatory networks are being decoded through inferred hub-miRNAs, which provide biologically informed recoding of transcriptomic data that consistently improves drug mechanism-of-action modeling [^2609.21280]. Pan-cancer co-expression networks are mapping hub genes that show strong potential for predicting differential drug responses across malignancies [^10.5281_zenodo.2574643]. Epigenetic profiling continues to yield diagnostic insights, with nucleosome repositioning landscapes in glioblastoma cell-free DNA offering potential biomarkers for non-invasive detection [^10.5526_err-00042862]. Multi-omic prognostic modeling frameworks are dissecting tumor-immune ecosystems across thousands of checkpoint blockade treatment cases to identify cellular programs predictive of therapeutic response [^10.5281_zenodo.18706180]. Finally, large-scale RNA-seq analyses in specific cohorts, such as Korean colorectal cancer populations, continue to refine molecular subtype classifications and highlight dysregulated metabolic and cell-cycle pathways [^10.5281_zenodo.8307739].
