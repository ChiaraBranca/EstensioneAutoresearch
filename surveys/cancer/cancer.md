# Living Survey: cancer

## Introduction

This document collects the scientific literature regarding **cancer**.

## Liquid Biopsy and Diagnostics
Recent advances in non-invasive cancer detection have focused heavily on cell-free DNA (cfDNA) and nucleosome positioning. Research has established comprehensive atlases of nucleosome positioning signatures across repetitive elements in tumor tissues and cfDNA, demonstrating that these signatures can robustly discriminate healthy individuals from patients with various cancers, including colorectal, pancreatic, and glioblastoma (GBM) [^10.5526_err-00042900]. Similarly, analysis of nucleosome repositioning in paired normal/tumor brain tissues and corresponding cfDNA from GBM patients has identified specific transcription factors as potential biomarkers, highlighting the clinical potential of liquid biopsies for early detection [^10.5526_err-00042862].

## Tumor Microenvironment and Metastasis
The spatial organization of the tumor microenvironment (TME) plays a critical role in metastasis and immune evasion. Single-cell and spatial multi-omics have revealed that clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) lung metastases harbor cancer-associated fibroblast lymphocyte exclusion sites that form a confined TME, directly impacting immune cell infiltration [^10.5281_zenodo.18767278][^10.5281_zenodo.18767279]. In parallel, large-scale spatially resolved atlases of pan-cancer brain metastases have been constructed, providing unprecedented insights into the cellular and spatial heterogeneity of metastatic niches across different primary tumor types [^10.5281_zenodo.21782884][^10.5281_zenodo.21782885][^10.5281_zenodo.21816284][^10.5281_zenodo.21816285].

## Metabolic and Nutritional Interventions
Metabolic reprogramming and nutritional interventions are emerging as potent strategies to induce cancer cell death. In prostate cancer, omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids (specifically EPA) have been shown to induce mitochondrial protein succinylation, disrupting mitochondrial homeostasis and triggering ferroptosis through GOT2 hyper-succinylation and SDHA modulation [^10.17638_03195029]. Similarly, in breast cancer, cyclic fasting and fasting-mimicking diets elevate plasma polyunsaturated fatty acids, which cooperate with nutrient starvation to induce lipid peroxidation and ferroptosis, offering a safe therapeutic strategy for triple-negative breast cancer [^10.21954_ou.ro.00108036].

## Genomic and Transcriptomic Profiling
High-throughput sequencing continues to unravel the molecular landscape of cancer. A pan-cancer co-expression network analysis has identified hub genes that hold significant potential for predicting drug responses across multiple malignancies [^10.5281_zenodo.2574643]. In colorectal cancer, comprehensive RNA-seq analysis of large cohorts has delineated differentially expressed genes enriched in cell cycle and DNA replication pathways, providing new therapeutic targets [^10.5281_zenodo.8307739]. Additionally, multi-omic prognostic modeling across thousands of checkpoint blockade treatment cases has enabled the systematic dissection of tumor-immune ecosystems and the identification of prognostic cellular programs [^10.5281_zenodo.18706179][^10.5281_zenodo.18706180].

## Therapeutic Targets and Drug Development
Novel therapeutic targets and drug development strategies are continuously being explored. In mesothelioma and head and neck cancer, deubiquitinating enzymes (DUBs) have been identified as key regulators of the Hippo pathway and YAP/TAZ activity, with USP42 emerging as a promising therapeutic target in mesothelioma [^10.17638_03193849]. The DEK oncoprotein has been characterized as a dynamic chromatin architectural factor that promotes metastatic melanoma progression through phosphorylation-dependent chromatin remodeling [^10.17638_03194520]. Chemical synthesis methods for mucin-type glycopeptides are being optimized to create homogeneous samples for cancer vaccine and CAR-T cell therapies [^10.48617_etd.1513]. Finally, non-genotoxic topoisomerase II catalytic inhibitors are being developed to offer alternative mechanisms for cancer therapy [^10.14288_1.0451818].

## Cancer Risk and Epidemiology
Environmental factors significantly influence cancer incidence. Recent epidemiological studies have investigated the association between the built environment and cancer risk, underscoring the importance of urban planning and environmental design in public health strategies [^10.14288_1.0451801].
