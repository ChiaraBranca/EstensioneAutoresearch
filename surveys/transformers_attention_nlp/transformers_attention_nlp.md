# Living Survey: transformers attention NLP

## Introduction

This document collects the scientific literature regarding **transformers attention NLP**.

## Pre-trained Language Models and Domain Adaptation

The year 2019 witnessed a surge in the application and refinement of pre-trained language models (PLMs) across diverse NLP tasks. The BERT architecture continued to dominate, with works like Pretrained Encyclopedia demonstrating that weakly supervised pretraining objectives explicitly incorporating real-world knowledge significantly outperform standard BERT on entity-related QA tasks [^10.48550_arxiv.1912.09637]. Similarly, AraNet leveraged bidirectional transformer encoders (BERT) to predict dialect, emotion, and sentiment in Arabic social media, delivering state-of-the-art results without manual feature engineering [^10.48550_arxiv.1912.13072]. The versatility of PLMs was further proven in clinical settings, where Clinical XLNet modeled sequential clinical notes to predict prolonged mechanical ventilation, outperforming baselines by leveraging temporal information [^10.48550_arxiv.1912.11975]. In the realm of summarization, leveraging lead bias for zero-shot abstractive news summarization showed that self-supervised pretraining on large unlabeled corpora using BART and T5 dramatically improved generation quality [^10.48550_arxiv.1912.11602]. Furthermore, history aggregation mechanisms based on Transformers were proposed to tackle long-text dependencies in abstractive summarization [^10.48550_arxiv.1912.11046]. Multilingual and language-independent models also showed promise, proving comparable to monolingual approaches and highly effective for low-resource languages [^10.48550_arxiv.1912.13106]. End-to-end joint models for NER and relation extraction demonstrated that integrating large pre-trained language models without external NLP tools matches or exceeds state-of-the-art performance [^10.48550_arxiv.1912.13415].

## Theoretical Foundations and Expressive Power

Beyond empirical applications, 2019 saw significant theoretical investigations into the fundamental properties of Transformer architectures. A pivotal study established that Transformer models are universal approximators of continuous permutation equivariant sequence-to-sequence functions with compact support [^10.48550_arxiv.1912.10077]. The research highlighted that fixed-width self-attention layers compute contextual mappings of input sequences, playing a crucial role in this universal approximation property, while positional encodings circumvent permutation equivariance restrictions. This theoretical grounding reinforced the architectural choices driving the NLP revolution.

## Architectural Innovations and Search Spaces

Parallel to theoretical work, novel architectural adaptations and search methodologies emerged. TextNAS introduced a neural architecture search space specifically tailored for text representation, automatically discovering networks that outperformed state-of-the-art models on classification and inference tasks [^10.48550_arxiv.1912.10729]. In parallel, complex-valued embeddings were proposed to model both global absolute positions and ordered relationships between words, extending CNN, RNN, and Transformer architectures to the complex domain to capture word order more effectively [^https:__openalex.org_W2997175890]. Domain-specific adaptations also flourished, with transformer-based models being successfully applied to extract structured chemical synthesis actions from unstructured experimental procedures [^10.26434_chemrxiv.11448177.v1]. Additionally, attention mechanisms continued to be refined for goal-oriented dialog systems, improving intent recognition and response adaptation under limited training data [^10.48550_arxiv.1912.10130].
