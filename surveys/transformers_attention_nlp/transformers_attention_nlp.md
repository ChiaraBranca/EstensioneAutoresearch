# Living Survey: transformers attention NLP

## Introduction

This document collects the scientific literature regarding **transformers attention NLP**.

## Pre-trained Language Models and Domain Adaptation

The year 2019 witnessed a surge in the application and refinement of pre-trained language models (PLMs) across diverse NLP tasks. The BERT architecture continued to dominate, with works like Pretrained Encyclopedia demonstrating that weakly supervised pretraining objectives explicitly incorporating real-world knowledge significantly outperform standard BERT on entity-related QA tasks [^10.48550_arxiv.1912.09637]. Similarly, AraNet leveraged bidirectional transformer encoders (BERT) to predict dialect, emotion, and sentiment in Arabic social media, delivering state-of-the-art results without manual feature engineering [^10.48550_arxiv.1912.13072]. The versatility of PLMs was further proven in clinical settings, where Clinical XLNet modeled sequential clinical notes to predict prolonged mechanical ventilation, outperforming baselines by leveraging temporal information [^10.48550_arxiv.1912.11975]. In the realm of summarization, leveraging lead bias for zero-shot abstractive news summarization showed that self-supervised pretraining on large unlabeled corpora using BART and T5 dramatically improved generation quality [^10.48550_arxiv.1912.11602]. Furthermore, history aggregation mechanisms based on Transformers were proposed to tackle long-text dependencies in abstractive summarization [^10.48550_arxiv.1912.11046]. Multilingual and language-independent models also showed promise, proving comparable to monolingual approaches and highly effective for low-resource languages [^10.48550_arxiv.1912.13106]. End-to-end joint models for NER and relation extraction demonstrated that integrating large pre-trained language models without external NLP tools matches or exceeds state-of-the-art performance [^10.48550_arxiv.1912.13415]. The exploration of self-supervised pre-training objectives tailored for specific downstream tasks advanced significantly. PEGASUS introduced a novel pre-training method that masks important sentences to generate extractive summaries, achieving state-of-the-art results across 12 diverse summarization datasets and demonstrating strong performance even in low-resource settings [^10.48550_arxiv.1912.08777].

## Theoretical Foundations and Expressive Power

Beyond empirical applications, 2019 saw significant theoretical investigations into the fundamental properties of Transformer architectures. A pivotal study established that Transformer models are universal approximators of continuous permutation equivariant sequence-to-sequence functions with compact support [^10.48550_arxiv.1912.10077]. The research highlighted that fixed-width self-attention layers compute contextual mappings of input sequences, playing a crucial role in this universal approximation property, while positional encodings circumvent permutation equivariance restrictions. This theoretical grounding reinforced the architectural choices driving the NLP revolution. Investigations into the expressive limits of neural representations revealed that while vector embeddings capture semantics well, they often struggle with pragmatic context. Research demonstrated that discourse marker prediction can serve as a crucial training signal to ground models in pragmatic reasoning, addressing the gap between literal semantics and meaning-as-use in current NLP evaluation suites [^https:__openalex.org_W3030025844].

## Architectural Innovations and Search Spaces

Parallel to theoretical work, novel architectural adaptations and search methodologies emerged. TextNAS introduced a neural architecture search space specifically tailored for text representation, automatically discovering networks that outperformed state-of-the-art models on classification and inference tasks [^10.48550_arxiv.1912.10729]. In parallel, complex-valued embeddings were proposed to model both global absolute positions and ordered relationships between words, extending CNN, RNN, and Transformer architectures to the complex domain to capture word order more effectively [^https:__openalex.org_W2997175890]. Domain-specific adaptations also flourished, with transformer-based models being successfully applied to extract structured chemical synthesis actions from unstructured experimental procedures [^10.26434_chemrxiv.11448177.v1]. Additionally, attention mechanisms continued to be refined for goal-oriented dialog systems, improving intent recognition and response adaptation under limited training data [^10.48550_arxiv.1912.10130]. Attention mechanisms were further refined to better capture inter-task dependencies and complex linguistic phenomena. A cyclic multitask learning framework was proposed for neural simile recognition, stacking subtasks in a loop connected by attention to iteratively exploit the mutual effects between sentence classification and component extraction, outperforming standard parameter-sharing baselines [^10.48550_arxiv.1912.09084]. Multimodal architectures also benefited from specialized attention designs. The Multimodal Target-Source Classifier with Attention Branches (MTCM-AB) extended attention networks to fuse linguistic and visual inputs, effectively resolving ambiguities in natural language instructions for robotic tasks and matching human-level accuracy [^10.48550_arxiv.1912.10675]. Hierarchical attention structures were adapted for interpretable recommendation systems. Asymmetrical Hierarchical Networks with Attentive Interactions were developed to process user and item reviews differently, learning to attend to heterogeneous user signals versus homogeneous item properties, thereby improving recommendation interpretability and accuracy [^10.48550_arxiv.2001.04346].

## Document-Level Context and Multilingual Adaptation

Moving beyond sentence-level processing, research increasingly focused on capturing long-range dependencies and document-level coherence. A comprehensive survey highlighted the shift from isolated sentence translation to neural models that incorporate wider document context and inter-sentence dependencies [^10.48550_arxiv.1912.08494]. Complementing this, recent work on document-level machine translation introduced context-aware decoding strategies and semantic space language models to resolve cross-sentence inconsistencies, such as gender/number disagreements and coreference chains, significantly improving translation quality for long texts [^10.5821_dissertation-2117-177244].

## Robustness and Adversarial NLP

The robustness of NLP models against adversarial perturbations was also scrutinized. The T3 framework introduced a tree-based autoencoder to map discrete text into continuous spaces for optimization, enabling targeted attacks on sentiment analysis and QA tasks while preserving syntactic correctness and human readability [^10.48550_arxiv.1912.10375].
