# Living Survey: Quantum_Computing

## Introduction

This document collects the scientific literature regarding **Quantum_Computing**.

## Quantum Computing Architectures and Foundations

Recent research continues to explore the fundamental boundaries of quantum computation and alternative paradigms. A unified framework connecting quantum phenomena, gravity, and computation has been proposed, redefining the quantum as a free particle emerging from energy interactions rather than an intrinsically discrete packet[^10.34257_gjsfra257362]. This reinterpretation highlights material scaling constraints in transistor computing and suggests that atom computing, utilizing controlled flows of single electrons or photons, may offer deterministic switching beyond current qubit-based systems[^10.34257_gjsfra257362]. Furthermore, comparative analyses of future computing paradigms continue to delineate the architectural distinctions between digital, quantum, and biological computers, emphasizing the need for standardized engineering descriptions to bridge theoretical potential with practical implementation[^10.1109_mc.2021.3083430].

## Quantum Software Engineering and Developer Ecosystems

The transition of quantum computing toward industrial adoption has spurred empirical investigations into developer engagement and toolchain maturity. An analysis of over 1,400 Stack Overflow posts reveals that hybrid quantum-classical computing and quantum circuit implementation dominate practitioner discussions, with Qiskit and Q-sharp emerging as the most referenced tools[^https:__openalex.org_W7155247427]. Grover's and Shor's algorithms remain the most frequently discussed algorithms, though varying levels of community support and question difficulty indicate uneven ecosystem maturity across different topics and tools[^https:__openalex.org_W7155247427]. Additionally, tracking emerging trends through open software repositories and financial news provides pivotal insights into the adoption trajectory and market dynamics of quantum technologies[^10.1007_978-3-031-90727-2_20].

## Quantum Cryptography and the Quantum Internet

Secure communication remains a cornerstone of quantum information science. Theoretical primers on the quantum internet emphasize its potential to transform global connectivity by enabling ultra-secure communication networks for quantum devices, moving beyond classical information transfer[^10.31695_ijerat.2025.9.1]. Practical implementations continue to be refined, with detailed academic explanations and Python simulations of the BB84 quantum key distribution (QKD) protocol demonstrating how quantum mechanics enables provably secure communication through state preparation, probabilistic measurement, and classical post-processing stages like sifting and privacy amplification[^10.6084_m9.figshare.32051724]. Concurrently, novel approaches in quantum cryptography are being developed to enhance data security, analyzing protocols such as BB84, B92, and B91, while addressing vulnerabilities like photon splitting, faked-state, and Trojan-horse attacks[^https:__openalex.org_W7112574406].

## Quantum Scientific Computing and Simulation

Quantum computing is increasingly applied to complex scientific simulations that are intractable for classical systems. In computational fluid dynamics (CFD), quantum algorithms based on the Lattice Boltzmann Method and Navier-Stokes equations are being explored for aeronautical heat exchangers and 2D flow simulations, demonstrating early-stage but promising applicability to industrial engineering problems[^10.11159_icffts23.133][^10.21741_9781644902813-38]. Beyond fluid dynamics, quantum annealing is being utilized for configurational analysis in materials science, providing practical guides for translating chemical structures and energy models into qubit representations to calculate relative energies of defective materials like graphene[^10.1063_5.0151346]. Furthermore, open-source software frameworks like PULSEE are bridging quantum information science with experimental research, simulating nuclear magnetic resonance observables and spin time evolution to investigate emergent quantum properties in strongly correlated materials[^10.1016_j.cpc.2022.108598].

## Post-Quantum Cryptography and Security Analysis

As quantum capabilities advance, the security of classical cryptographic systems faces unprecedented challenges. Comprehensive reviews of quantum computing applications continue to map out the prospective disruptions and stakeholder impacts of widespread quantum adoption[^10.5120_ijca2021921834]. In response, rigorous cryptanalysis is being conducted against leading post-quantum key exchange and signature schemes. Improved dual lattice attacks against Learning With Errors (LWE) and Learning With Rounding (LWR) problems have demonstrated algorithmic enhancements that significantly reduce the estimated security levels of NIST finalists like Kyber, Saber, and Dilithium, bringing them below previously defined thresholds[^10.5281_zenodo.6493704]. Concurrently, advanced hybrid encryption systems are being proposed to mitigate these threats, combining classical ciphers with dynamic compression and dual authentication mechanisms while explicitly accounting for the computational impacts of Grover’s and Shor’s algorithms[^10.56975_tijer.v13i3.161368].
