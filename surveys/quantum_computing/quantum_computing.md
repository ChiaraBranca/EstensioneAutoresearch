# Living Survey: Quantum_Computing

## Introduction

This document collects the scientific literature regarding **Quantum_Computing**.

## Quantum Hardware and Architectures

Recent research continues to explore novel computational paradigms beyond traditional transistor and qubit systems. A unified framework redefining matter and particle behavior proposes atom computing as a deterministic alternative, utilizing controlled flows of single electrons or photons to overcome material scaling constraints[^10.34257_gjsfra257362]. Furthermore, comparative studies provide standardized architectural models for digital, quantum, and biological computers, highlighting the immense performance potential of quantum systems while establishing baseline engineering descriptions[^10.1109_mc.2021.3083430].

## Quantum Software, Toolchains, and Simulation

The ecosystem surrounding quantum software development is maturing, with empirical analyses of developer communities revealing that hybrid quantum-classical computing and circuit implementation dominate practitioner discussions, alongside tools like Qiskit and Q-sharp[^https:__openalex.org_W7155247427]. To support research, open-source frameworks like PULSEE have been developed to simulate extensive magnetic resonance observables and quantum gate time-evolutions, bridging quantum information science with condensed matter physics[^10.1016_j.cpc.2022.108598]. Additionally, hybrid quantum-classical granular computing approaches are being introduced to optimize continuous search spaces, statistically enhancing evolutionary algorithms on limited-qubit processors[^10.1007_s41066-022-00348-9].

## Quantum Communication and Cryptography

Secure quantum communication remains a pivotal area of investigation. Practical implementations of the BB84 quantum key distribution (QKD) protocol have been simulated in Python, modeling state preparation, eavesdropping attacks, and classical post-processing to demonstrate provably secure communication[^10.6084_m9.figshare.32051724]. Broader overviews of quantum cryptography detail foundational protocols (BB84, B92, B91) and analyze vulnerabilities such as photon splitting and faked-state attacks, alongside the implications of Shor's algorithm[^https:__openalex.org_W7112574406]. The theoretical foundation for a global quantum Internet is also being established, outlining protocols for ultra-secure connectivity and large-scale quantum device networks[^10.31695_ijerat.2025.9.1]. Concurrently, the cryptographic landscape is adapting to quantum threats; recent cryptanalysis on Learning With Errors (LWE) problems demonstrates improved dual lattice attacks that significantly reduce the security margins of NIST post-quantum cryptography finalists like Kyber and Dilithium[^10.5281_zenodo.6493704]. In response, advanced hybrid encryption systems incorporating post-quantum considerations and benchmarking against quantum algorithms like Grover's and Shor's are being developed[^10.56975_tijer.v13i3.161368].

## Quantum Applications in Science and Industry

Quantum computing is increasingly applied to complex scientific and industrial problems. In computational fluid dynamics (CFD), quantum algorithms based on Navier-Stokes equations and the Lattice Boltzmann Method are being reviewed and implemented to simulate aeronautical heat exchangers and 2D fluid flows[^10.11159_icffts23.133] [^10.21741_9781644902813-38]. In materials science, practical guides for quantum annealing enable the configurational analysis of defective structures, such as graphene, translating chemical models into qubit representations for energy calculations[^10.1063_5.0151346]. Finally, tracking emerging technological trends through open repositories and financial news provides valuable insights into the industrial adoption trajectory of quantum technologies[^10.1007_978-3-031-90727-2_20].
