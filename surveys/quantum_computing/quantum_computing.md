# Living Survey: Quantum_Computing

## Introduction

This document collects the scientific literature regarding **Quantum_Computing**.

## Recent Advances and Applications

The field continues to expand into novel computational paradigms and practical implementations. Recent work proposes **atom computing** as a successor to transistor-based architectures, suggesting that controlled flows of single electrons or photons could overcome current scaling limits by redefining matter and energy interactions [^10.34257_gjsfra257362]. In parallel, educational and simulation tools are bridging the gap between theory and practice, such as Python-based simulations of the BB84 quantum key distribution protocol that model state preparation, eavesdropping, and classical post-processing [^10.6084_m9.figshare.32051724].

Developer ecosystems and software engineering around quantum technologies are also maturing. An empirical analysis of developer discussions reveals that hybrid quantum-classical computing and circuit implementation dominate community engagement, with Qiskit and Q-sharp being the most referenced tools [^https:__openalex.org_W7155247427]. To support this growing ecosystem, new hybrid iterative cipher chaining systems like CypherGliph have been benchmarked across classical, GPU/FPGA, and quantum computing platforms to evaluate post-quantum cryptographic resilience [^10.56975_tijer.v13i3.161368].

## Quantum Cryptography and Security

Securing communications against future quantum threats remains a critical research vector. A new cryptographic approach details the implementation of BB84, B92, and B91 protocols alongside Shor's algorithm on quantum hardware to enhance data security [^https:__openalex.org_W7112574406]. Concurrently, cryptanalysis research has introduced algorithmic improvements to dual lattice attacks against Learning With Errors (LWE), demonstrating that these attacks can now exceed the efficiency of primal attacks and significantly reduce the estimated security levels of NIST post-quantum cryptography finalists like Kyber and Dilithium [^10.5281_zenodo.6493704].

## Quantum Internet and Network Architectures

Beyond individual processors, the vision of a global quantum network is gaining theoretical traction. Recent primers outline the foundational protocols for a quantum Internet, emphasizing its potential to provide ultra-secure connectivity and transform industries by transmitting information encoded in quantum states across distributed quantum devices [^10.31695_ijerat.2025.9.1]. Tracking the adoption and financial trends of such emerging technologies is also becoming data-driven, with studies leveraging GitHub repositories and financial news to map quantum technology trajectories [^10.1007_978-3-031-90727-2_20].

## Quantum Simulation and Scientific Computing

Quantum computing is increasingly applied to complex scientific simulations. In computational fluid dynamics (CFD), researchers are exploring quantum algorithms based on the Lattice Boltzmann Method and Navier-Stokes equations to simulate 2D flows and aeronautical heat exchangers, marking an early but promising stage of development [^10.11159_icffts23.133][^10.21741_9781644902813-38]. Similarly, quantum annealing is being utilized for configurational analysis in materials science, providing a practical guide for calculating the relative energies of defective graphene structures [^10.1063_5.0151346]. Furthermore, open-source software like PULSEE leverages quantum simulation techniques to model nuclear magnetic resonance observables, bridging condensed matter physics and quantum information science [^10.1016_j.cpc.2022.108598].

## Quantum Algorithms and Optimization

Algorithmic efficiency on near-term quantum hardware is a major focus. Hybrid approaches combining granular computing with quantum processors have been developed to optimize continuous functions, allowing digital computers to handle fitness evaluations while quantum devices evolve genetic populations [^10.1007_s41066-022-00348-9].

## Historical Context and Architectural Comparisons

Foundational reviews continue to clarify the landscape. Early comparative studies have provided standardized architectural descriptions of digital, quantum, and biological computers to contextualize quantum performance potential [^10.1109_mc.2021.3083430]. Comprehensive overviews also continue to explore the core principles of superposition and entanglement, mapping out prospective applications and industry disruptions [^10.5120_ijca2021921834].
