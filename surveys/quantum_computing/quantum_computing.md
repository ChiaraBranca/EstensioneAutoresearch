# Living Survey: Quantum_Computing

## Introduction

This document collects the scientific literature regarding **Quantum_Computing**.

## Recent Advances and Applications (2021–2026)

### Quantum Algorithms and Simulation
Recent years have seen significant progress in quantum simulation and algorithmic optimization. The PULSEE software package provides an open-source framework for simulating magnetic resonance observables, bridging quantum information science with condensed matter physics [^10.1016_j.cpc.2022.108598]. In the realm of optimization, granular computing has been successfully hybridized with quantum processors to enhance genetic optimization algorithms, demonstrating improved performance on continuous benchmark functions [^10.1007_s41066-022-00348-9]. Furthermore, quantum annealing has been practically applied to materials science, offering a step-by-step guide for calculating the relative energy of defective graphene structures [^10.1063_5.0151346]. Early reviews continue to shape the field, outlining the core principles of superposition and entanglement while mapping prospective applications and stakeholder impacts [^10.5120_ijca2021921834].

### Quantum Software, Toolchains, and Ecosystem Trends
Understanding developer engagement and ecosystem trends is crucial for the maturation of quantum technologies. An empirical analysis of Stack Overflow data reveals that hybrid quantum-classical computing and circuit implementation dominate developer discussions, with Qiskit and Q-sharp being the most referenced tools [^https:__openalex.org_W7155247427]. Tracking emerging trends through open-source repositories and financial news highlights the growing intersection of quantum research and industrial investment [^10.1007_978-3-031-90727-2_20]. To bridge theory and practice, academic simulations of the BB84 quantum key distribution protocol have been published, detailing state preparation, eavesdropping models, and classical post-processing stages [^10.6084_m9.figshare.32051724].

### Quantum Cryptography and Security
As quantum capabilities advance, cryptographic security and post-quantum transitions remain critical research areas. The concept of the Quantum Internet is gaining traction as a theoretical yet transformative network for ultra-secure connectivity and distributed quantum computing [^10.31695_ijerat.2025.9.1]. New approaches to quantum cryptography are being developed to enhance data security against evolving threats [^https:__openalex.org_W7112574406]. On the post-quantum front, improved dual lattice attacks have been demonstrated against Learning With Errors (LWE) based schemes, significantly reducing the estimated security levels of finalists like Kyber and Dilithium and prompting ongoing refinement of post-quantum standards [^10.5281_zenodo.6493704]. Additionally, novel hybrid encryption systems integrating AES-GCM, ChaCha20, and Poly1305 are being designed with explicit considerations for Grover’s and Shor’s algorithmic threats [^10.56975_tijer.v13i3.161368].

### Quantum Applications in Engineering and Materials
Quantum computing is increasingly being explored for complex computational fluid dynamics (CFD) problems. State-of-the-art reviews highlight the early but promising stage of quantum algorithms applied to CFD, including Lattice Boltzmann Method implementations for simulating 2D flows around cylinders [^10.21741_9781644902813-38]. Specific applications to aeronautical heat exchangers suggest that quantum algorithms and analog quantum computers could eventually solve Navier-Stokes equations more efficiently than classical counterparts [^10.11159_icffts23.133].

### Future Architectures and Atom Computing
Looking beyond traditional transistor and qubit paradigms, new computational models are emerging. A recent framework proposes "atom computing," where controlled flows of single electrons or photons replace transistors, potentially overcoming material scaling limits [^10.34257_gjsfra257362]. Broader architectural comparisons continue to evaluate the distinct advantages of digital, quantum, and biological computing systems, emphasizing the need for standardized engineering descriptions to guide future development [^10.1109_mc.2021.3083430].
