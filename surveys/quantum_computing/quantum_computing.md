# Living Survey: Quantum_Computing

## Introduction

This document collects the scientific literature regarding **Quantum_Computing**.

## Quantum Algorithms & Optimization

Recent research continues to explore the intersection of quantum mechanics and optimization algorithms. Granular computing has been proposed to hierarchically reduce search spaces, allowing quantum genetic optimization to identify near-optimal solutions even on limited-qubit processors [^10.1007_s41066-022-00348-9]. Furthermore, foundational reviews emphasize the counterintuitive nature of quantum bits (qubits) and their potential to disrupt conventional digital logic through superposition and entanglement [^10.5120_ijca2021921834].

## Quantum Simulation & Materials Science

The application of quantum computers to computational chemistry and materials science is advancing rapidly. A practical guide demonstrates how quantum annealers can calculate the relative energy of defective graphene structures, translating chemical models into qubit representations [^10.1063_5.0151346]. Additionally, specialized software like PULSEE has been developed to simulate nuclear spin dynamics and magnetic resonance observables, bridging quantum information science with condensed matter research [^10.1016_j.cpc.2022.108598].

## Quantum Cryptography & Communication

Secure communication remains a primary driver for quantum research. A new approach to quantum cryptography outlines protocols such as BB84, B92, and B91, alongside analyses of Shor's algorithm and various attack vectors like photon splitting and faked-state attacks [^https:__openalex.org_W7112574406]. Practical implementations are also being documented, including detailed Python simulations of the BB84 protocol that model state preparation, eavesdropping, and privacy amplification [^10.6084_m9.figshare.32051724]. The broader vision of a Quantum Internet is also gaining traction, with studies providing primers on how quantum networks will transform global connectivity and security [^10.31695_ijerat.2025.9.1].

## Quantum Computing Applications in Engineering

Beyond theory, quantum computing is being applied to complex engineering problems. Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) simulations for aeronautical heat exchangers are being explored through quantum algorithms, including approaches based on the Lattice Boltzmann Method and scientific machine learning [^10.11159_icffts23.133][^10.21741_9781644902813-38]. While still in early development stages, these applications highlight the potential for quantum architectures to solve high-dimensional fluid dynamics problems.

## Post-Quantum Cryptography & Security

As quantum capabilities grow, the security of classical cryptographic systems is under scrutiny. Research into the Learning With Errors (LWE) problem has led to improved dual lattice attacks that challenge the security assumptions of post-quantum finalists like Kyber, Saber, and Dilithium [^10.5281_zenodo.6493704]. Concurrently, hybrid encryption systems are being designed to withstand quantum threats, incorporating dynamic compression and dual authentication mechanisms alongside post-quantum cryptographic strategies [^10.56975_tijer.v13i3.161368].

## Future Architectures & Paradigms

The landscape of future computing extends beyond traditional digital and quantum models. Analyses of open-source repositories and financial news indicate emerging trends in quantum software ecosystems [^10.1007_978-3-031-90727-2_20]. Alternative paradigms, such as atom computing, are proposed to overcome the material scaling constraints of transistor-based systems and the conceptual limitations of current qubit architectures, utilizing controlled electron or photon flows for deterministic switching [^10.34257_gjsfra257362]. Comparative studies continue to provide layperson-friendly architectural descriptions of digital, quantum, and biological computing systems to standardize industry models [^10.1109_mc.2021.3083430].
