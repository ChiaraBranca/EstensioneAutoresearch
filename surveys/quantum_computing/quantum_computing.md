# Living Survey: Quantum_Computing

## Introduction

This document collects the scientific literature regarding **Quantum_Computing**.

## Quantum Algorithms and Optimization
Recent research continues to refine quantum algorithms for complex optimization problems. Acampora and Vitiello introduced a hybrid granular computing approach to quantum genetic optimization, demonstrating that hierarchically reducing search spaces allows near-optimal solutions to be identified even on current limited-qubit processors [^10.1007_s41066-022-00348-9]. Similarly, Malinverno and colleagues have explored the application of quantum algorithms to computational fluid dynamics (CFD), specifically utilizing the Lattice Boltzmann Method to simulate 2D flows around cylinders, highlighting both the promising applicability and inherent implementation difficulties of quantum approaches in engineering simulations [^10.11159_icffts23.133][^10.21741_9781644902813-38]. Jun formulated QUBO models for solving linear systems by leveraging linear least-square problems with binary representations, validating these approaches on D-Wave quantum annealers to demonstrate practical utility for numerical computing [^10.48550_arxiv.2106.10819]. Additionally, Ghosh introduced QGameTheory, an R package providing a comprehensive simulation toolbox for quantum game theory, enabling the modeling of classical strategic interactions like the Prisoner's Dilemma and Monty Hall Problem within quantum frameworks [^10.32614_cran.package.qgametheory].

## Quantum Simulation and Software Ecosystems
The development of robust software tools is critical for advancing quantum simulation capabilities. Candoli et al. presented PULSEE, an open-source Python package that bridges magnetic resonance spectroscopy and quantum information science by simulating spin time evolution and quantum gates, facilitating the study of strongly correlated quantum materials [^10.1016_j.cpc.2022.108598]. In the domain of materials science, Camino et al. provided a practical guide to applying quantum annealing to configurational analysis, demonstrating how defective graphene structures can be modeled and evaluated on D-Wave annealers to solve problems intractable for classical computers [^10.1063_5.0151346].

## Quantum Communication and Cryptographic Implications
Quantum communication networks and their cryptographic foundations remain a highly active research frontier. Sadiku et al. outlined the theoretical framework of the quantum internet, positioning it as a transformative infrastructure for ultra-secure connectivity and quantum device interconnectivity [^10.31695_ijerat.2025.9.1]. Building on protocol implementations, Badiezadegan detailed a comprehensive Python simulation of the BB84 QKD protocol, modeling state preparation, intercept-resend attacks, and classical post-processing to bridge theoretical QIS with practical secure communication [^10.6084_m9.figshare.32051724]. Kastanoulas further compared foundational quantum cryptography protocols (BB84, B92, B91) and analyzed security threats like photon splitting and faked-state attacks [^https:__openalex.org_W7112574406]. Conversely, the advent of large-scale quantum computers poses direct threats to classical and post-quantum cryptography. MATZOV reported significant algorithmic improvements to dual lattice attacks against Learning With Errors (LWE) schemes, demonstrating that these attacks can now exceed the efficiency of primal attacks and reduce the security margins of NIST PQC finalists like Kyber and Dilithium [^10.5281_zenodo.6493704]. Pandya et al. addressed this by proposing a hybrid encryption system that integrates dynamic compression and dual authentication, while discussing the impact of Grover’s and Shor’s algorithms and outlining strategies for post-quantum cryptography integration [^10.56975_tijer.v13i3.161368]. Gidney and Ekerå significantly optimized Shor's algorithm, estimating that factoring 2048-bit RSA integers could be achieved in just 8 hours using 20 million noisy qubits, while thoroughly accounting for noise, error correction overheads, and spacetime layout to quantify the imminent threat to classical cryptographic standards [^10.22331_q-2021-04-15-433].

## Foundational Perspectives and Future Architectures
Foundational reviews continue to shape the understanding of quantum computing's trajectory. Jayaram and Adavi provided an accessible exposition of quantum percepts, emphasizing superposition and entanglement as the hallmarks distinguishing quantum bits from classical digital logic [^10.5120_ijca2021921834]. Looking toward broader computational paradigms, Treleaven offered comparative architectural descriptions of digital, quantum, and biological computers, advocating for standardized engineering models to facilitate industry adoption [^10.1109_mc.2021.3083430].
