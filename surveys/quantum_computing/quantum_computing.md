# Living Survey: Quantum_Computing

## Introduction

This document collects the scientific literature regarding **Quantum_Computing**.

## Quantum Algorithms and Simulation

Recent advancements have significantly expanded the toolkit for simulating quantum systems and optimizing quantum algorithms. The PULSEE software package introduces an open-source framework for simulating nuclear spin ensemble evolution, bridging the gap between quantum information science and magnetic resonance spectroscopy by providing extensive libraries for spin time-evolution and quantum gate simulation[^10.1016_j.cpc.2022.108598]. In the realm of optimization, hybrid and granular computing approaches have been proposed to overcome qubit limitations in continuous optimization problems. By hierarchically reducing search spaces, these methods statistically enhance the performance of evolutionary algorithms on current quantum processors[^10.1007_s41066-022-00348-9]. Furthermore, practical guides for quantum annealing have emerged, demonstrating how chemical structures and energy models can be translated into qubit representations for configurational analysis of materials like defective graphene[^10.1063_5.0151346].

## Quantum Communication and Cryptography

Secure quantum communication remains a cornerstone of the quantum information landscape. A detailed Python simulation of the BB84 Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) protocol has been presented, modeling state preparation, Born rule-based measurement, intercept-resend attacks, and classical post-processing stages to bridge theoretical concepts with practical implementation[^10.6084_m9.figshare.32051724]. Expanding on cryptographic foundations, a new approach to quantum cryptography has been proposed to enhance data security, analyzing protocols such as BB84, B92, and B91 alongside attacks like photon splitting and faked-state attacks[^https:__openalex.org_W7112574406]. The broader infrastructure for quantum networks is also being defined, with primers on the Quantum Internet outlining how global networks of quantum devices will enable ultra-secure connectivity and transform information transmission[^10.31695_ijerat.2025.9.1]. Concurrently, the security landscape for post-quantum cryptography is being rigorously tested; improved dual lattice attacks against Learning With Errors (LWE) problems have been developed, demonstrating reduced security margins for leading NIST finalists like Kyber and Dilithium[^10.5281_zenodo.6493704].

## Quantum Applications in Engineering and Science

The application of quantum computing to complex engineering and scientific simulations is rapidly maturing. In computational fluid dynamics (CFD), quantum algorithms based on the Lattice Boltzmann Method are being explored for simulating 2D flows around cylinders and modeling aeronautical heat exchangers, highlighting both the promise and implementation challenges of quantum-accelerated simulations[^10.11159_icffts23.133][^10.21741_9781644902813-38]. Beyond fluid dynamics, quantum computing is being positioned as a transformative paradigm across multiple computing architectures, with comparative analyses providing standardized models for digital, quantum, and biological computers to guide industry adoption[^10.1109_mc.2021.3083430]. Broader surveys continue to map the percepts and realms of quantum applications, emphasizing the disruptive potential of superposition and entanglement in solving previously intractable problems[^10.5120_ijca2021921834].

## Quantum Ecosystem and Architecture

Understanding the developer ecosystem and architectural alternatives is crucial for the long-term trajectory of quantum technologies. An empirical investigation mining Stack Overflow repositories reveals that hybrid quantum-classical computing and circuit implementation dominate developer discussions, with Qiskit and Q-sharp leading tool usage, while Grover's and Shor's algorithms remain the most referenced[^https:__openalex.org_W7155247427]. Tracking these trends through open software repositories and financial news provides valuable insights into the emerging quantum technology landscape[^10.1007_978-3-031-90727-2_20]. Finally, theoretical physics continues to inform computational paradigms; a unified framework connecting quantum phenomena, gravity, and computation proposes "atom computing" as a deterministic alternative to transistor and qubit-based systems, suggesting that controlled flows of single electrons or photons may overcome current material scaling constraints[^10.34257_gjsfra257362].
