# Living Survey: Quantum_Computing

## Introduction

This document collects the scientific literature regarding **Quantum_Computing**.

## Quantum Software Ecosystems and Algorithmic Optimization
Recent studies have focused on the practical deployment and optimization of quantum algorithms. An empirical investigation into developer discussions on Stack Overflow reveals that hybrid quantum-classical computing and circuit implementation are the most prevalent topics, with Qiskit and Q-sharp dominating the ecosystem alongside frequent references to Grover's and Shor's algorithms [^https:__openalex.org_W7155247427]. In the realm of optimization, hybrid granular computing approaches have been shown to statistically enhance evolutionary algorithms on current IBM Q processors, addressing the limitations of qubit counts in continuous optimization problems [^10.1007_s41066-022-00348-9]. Furthermore, specialized software like PULSEE has emerged to simulate nuclear spin ensemble evolution, bridging quantum information science with magnetic resonance experiments for investigating emergent quantum phases in materials [^10.1016_j.cpc.2022.108598]. Quantum annealing is also being practically applied to configurational analysis, providing a guide for translating chemical structures into qubit representations for D-Wave processors [^10.1063_5.0151346].

## Quantum Cryptography and Secure Communication
The intersection of quantum mechanics and cybersecurity continues to yield significant advancements. A detailed simulation of the BB84 quantum key distribution (QKD) protocol has been implemented to model state preparation, eavesdropping attacks, and classical post-processing, effectively bridging theoretical quantum mechanics with practical secure communication [^10.6084_m9.figshare.32051724]. This aligns with broader research into quantum cryptography, including analyses of BB84, B92, and Shor's algorithm implementations on quantum computers [^https:__openalex.org_W7112574406]. The concept of the Quantum Internet is also gaining traction, promising ultra-secure connectivity and a network of quantum computers capable of transmitting information encoded in quantum states [^10.31695_ijerat.2025.9.1]. On the defensive side, new encryption frameworks like CypherGliph integrate dynamic compression and dual authentication, while explicitly benchmarking resistance against Grover’s and Shor’s algorithms on classical, GPU, and quantum platforms [^10.56975_tijer.v13i3.161368]. Additionally, cryptanalysis reports highlight that improved dual lattice attacks significantly reduce the security margins of post-quantum finalists like Kyber and Dilithium, emphasizing the need for rigorous security evaluation [^10.5281_zenodo.6493704].

## Emerging Hardware Paradigms and Computational Applications
Beyond software, novel hardware architectures and cross-domain applications are being explored. A unified framework proposes "atom computing," where controlled flows of single electrons or photons replace transistors to overcome material scaling constraints, positioning itself as an alternative to current qubit-based systems [^10.34257_gjsfra257362]. In engineering, quantum computing is being applied to computational fluid dynamics (CFD) for aeronautical heat exchangers, utilizing approaches like the Lattice Boltzmann Method and scientific machine learning, though it remains in an early developmental stage [^10.11159_icffts23.133][^10.21741_9781644902813-38]. Broader comparative analyses continue to examine the architectural differences between digital, quantum, and biological computers, providing standardized models for industry adoption [^10.1109_mc.2021.3083430]. Tracking these developments, recent analyses of open-source repositories and financial news demonstrate the pivotal role of community-driven platforms in monitoring emerging quantum trends [^10.1007_978-3-031-90727-2_20].
