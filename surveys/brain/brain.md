# Living Survey: brain

## Introduction

This document collects the scientific literature regarding **brain**.

## Brain-Computer Interfaces and Neurophysiology
Recent advancements in EEG and MEG signal processing have significantly improved brain-computer interface (BCI) performance and cortical mapping. Machine learning approaches, particularly Random Forest algorithms, have proven effective for classifying EEG signals during real and imagery motor activities, even with consumer-grade hardware [^2609.11695]. To address reproducibility in neuronal avalanche analysis, pyAvalanches provides a standardized Python pipeline for characterizing spatiotemporal propagation in EEG recordings [^2609.11530]. In P300 spellers, component anti-synergy has been observed, demonstrating that combining multiple filtering and calibration techniques does not always yield additive performance gains, emphasizing the need for tailored pipeline design [^2609.10961]. Furthermore, communication-aware federated personalization frameworks like NEXUS-MI optimize synchronization and backbone updates for motor-imagery BCIs, reducing server-client traffic while maintaining accuracy [^2609.09786]. Source-level analysis of interictal epileptiform discharges has also been advanced through EEG-centric multimodal binding, improving classification robustness by treating video context as auxiliary evidence [^2609.09728]. Cortical information transfer studies using Permutation Transfer Entropy reveal that hemispherical network dynamics during motor execution are conserved across handedness, challenging assumptions of mirrored lateralization [^2609.10870]. Additionally, infant temperament research links EEG microstates to regulatory capacity and emotional reactivity, expanding our understanding of early neurodevelopment [^10.7273_000007929].

## Computational Neuroscience and AI-Driven Brain Modeling
The intersection of AI and neuroscience continues to yield novel architectures for brain modeling and decoding. CoMA-DiT introduces a bidirectional cross-modal Diffusion Transformer for latent augmentation, treating paired modalities as mutual generative supervision to enrich multimodal brain state decoding [^2609.11341]. The "Platonic brain bridge hypothesis" proposes that omni models processing video, audio, and text converge on brain-like representations, with cortical networks serving as architectural priors that significantly boost benchmark accuracy [^2609.10947]. For fMRI foundation models, BrainTaskonomy organizes pretraining and adaptation through measured learning relations, using a priority-guided curriculum and integer programming to select source tasks, thereby reducing prediction error and revealing asymmetric transfer patterns [^2609.10518]. Covariance neural networks (VNNs) operating on graph structures provide theoretical stability bounds and transferability insights for characterizing brain age gaps in neurodegenerative conditions [^2609.10490]. Quantum reservoir computing utilizing coherent Floquet evolution has also been applied to predict molecular properties and blood-brain barrier permeability, showing robustness under device noise [^2609.11071]. Furthermore, an automated explanation-knowledge loop (XAI-Refine) iteratively refines brain-age prediction models by aligning post-hoc explanations with verified neurobiological literature [^2609.09388].

## Brain Tumors and Metastases
Clinical research on brain tumors continues to leverage advanced imaging and molecular profiling. For brain metastases, a pragmatic nnU-Net ResEnc-L ensemble combined with rule-based post-processing achieves high lesion-wise Dice similarity coefficients, though out-of-fold audits reveal that some leaderboard gains do not generalize [^2609.11477]. Scale-aware 3D deep learning frameworks combining different spatial fields of view improve lesion-level precision and reduce false positives in multimodal MRI detection [^2609.10825]. In glioblastoma (GBM), liquid biopsy approaches analyzing cell-free DNA (cfDNA) nucleosome positioning offer non-invasive diagnostic potential, with repeat-based cfDNA profiling distinguishing patients from healthy controls [^10.5526_err-00042862][^10.5526_err-00042900]. Secretomes and extracellular vesicles (EVs) derived from neural crest stem cells inhibit GBM proliferation and modulate NF-κB signaling, offering a novel immunomodulatory therapeutic avenue [^10.48683_1926.00128572]. Additionally, spatially resolved atlases of pan-cancer brain metastases and RET signaling pathways in estrogen receptor-positive breast cancer brain metastasis provide critical single-cell and molecular insights [^10.5281_zenodo.21816284][^10.5281_zenodo.21816285][^10.5281_zenodo.21782885][^10.5281_zenodo.21782884][^10.5281_zenodo.19501083][^10.5281_zenodo.19501082].

## Neuroimaging and Registration
High-resolution neuroimaging and registration techniques are advancing rapidly. A two-parameter flow map learning framework directly learns continuous-time non-autonomous ODE solutions for diffeomorphic image registration, improving alignment accuracy on brain MRI benchmarks without time discretization [^2609.10789]. Deep learning pipelines now enable fast, accurate monomodal 3D registration of Drosophila larval brain volumes, outperforming classical methods in both speed and landmark-local mutual information [^2609.11240]. LUCID combines multi-view diffusion priors with projection-domain data consistency to resolve missing-cone artifacts in laminographic X-ray nanoimaging of mammalian brains [^2609.10456]. A regression-based framework utilizing Wendland radial basis functions and model-based gradient boosting provides spatially resolved cortical activation estimates from MEG data, mitigating multiple testing corrections [^2609.11278]. Furthermore, comprehensive arteriovenous metabolic profiling across human organs, including the brain, reveals extensive inter-organ metabolite exchange, establishing a quantitative atlas for pathological feature identification [^10.5281_zenodo.20652506][^10.5281_zenodo.20652505].

## Neurobiology, Aging, and Metabolism
Fundamental neurobiological and aging research continues to expand our understanding of brain function. Brain-PACE extends longitudinal Siamese frameworks to directly estimate structural brain aging pace from paired T1-weighted MRI, identifying accelerated aging in mild cognitive impairment and correlating with tau burden [^2609.11378]. The emergence of criticality in large-scale neural models suggests that fine-tuning is unnecessary, as parameter space naturally concentrates near critical points as system size grows [^2609.09438]. In Drosophila, circular RNAs (circRNAs) accumulate in the brain at rates corresponding to biological age, serving as stable aging biomarkers [^10.48617_etd.1568]. Olfactory receptor choice is governed by a spatial code that aligns sensory maps in the nose and brain via retinoic acid signaling gradients [^10.5281_zenodo.18941307][^10.5281_zenodo.18941308]. Cholecystokinin (CCK) signaling in the nucleus of the solitary tract (NTS) is blunted by high-fat diets, linking gut-brain vagal afferents to satiety and obesity [^10.7273_000008411]. Transcutaneous cervical vagus nerve stimulation (tcVNS) demonstrates feasibility as a low-attention implicit intervention for reducing eating behavior [^2609.09700]. Finally, the amyloid precursor protein (APP) is re-evaluated as a universal survival molecule, arguing against broad-spectrum APP suppression therapies in Alzheimer's disease [^10.5281_zenodo.20049820]. Category fluency performance is further shown to be influenced by gray matter volumes in the DLPFC and ITG, with cultural and age-related structural variations playing significant roles [^10.48617_etd.1671]. Topology-first frameworks like SkNeXt enable scalable neuronal reconstruction from petabyte-scale microscopy data, while explainable ML frameworks predict blood-brain barrier permeability using molecular descriptors [^2609.09832][^2609.10012].
