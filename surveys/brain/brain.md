# Living Survey: brain

## Introduction

This document collects the scientific literature regarding **brain**.

## Brain-Computer Interfaces and Neurophysiological Signal Processing

Recent advancements in brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) and neurophysiological signal processing have focused on improving classification accuracy, personalization, and reproducibility. Machine learning approaches, particularly Random Forest algorithms, have demonstrated robust performance in classifying EEG signals from real and imagery motor activities, even with consumer-grade hardware, though interpersonal variability remains a challenge [^2609.11695]. To standardize the analysis of brain dynamics, the open-source package pyAvalanches provides a complete pipeline for characterizing neuronal avalanches and their spatiotemporal propagation using EEG recordings [^2609.11530]. In the domain of P300 spellers, factorial experiments have revealed component anti-synergy, showing that combining individually promising filters and language models does not always yield additive performance gains, emphasizing the need for context-aware pipeline design [^2609.10961]. Federated learning frameworks like NEXUS-MI address the personalization bottleneck in motor-imagery BCIs by coordinating edge devices while minimizing communication overhead [^2609.09786]. Furthermore, multimodal binding strategies such as EEGBind leverage synchronized video context to improve source-level classification of interictal epileptiform discharges from subtle EEG windows [^2609.09728]. Beyond clinical applications, brain-based digital twins are being developed to fuse consumer-grade EEG with kinematic data for real-time feedback in skilled motor tasks like cricket batting [^10.17870_bathspa.00017805], while infant EEG microstate analysis continues to uncover links between early temperament traits and global brain electrical activity [^10.7273_000007929].

## Advanced Neuroimaging and Metastasis Detection

Volumetric segmentation and registration of brain structures continue to be driven by deep learning ensembles and diffusion models. For brain metastases, an nnU-Net ResEnc-L ensemble combined with rule-based post-processing has achieved state-of-the-art lesion-wise Dice scores on the BraTS 2026 benchmark, with rigorous out-of-fold validation confirming the robustness of specific processing stages [^2609.11477]. Scale-aware 3D deep learning frameworks utilizing cross-field-of-view probability fusion have also proven effective in reducing false positives while maintaining high precision in multimodal MRI metastasis detection [^2609.10825]. In developmental neuroscience, a trained deep registration pipeline now aligns Drosophila larval brain volumes to a reference template in a single forward pass, offering orders-of-magnitude speed improvements over classical deformable methods [^2609.11240]. Diffeomorphic image registration has been advanced through a two-parameter flow map learning framework that enforces cocycle consistency, yielding improved alignment accuracy on brain MRI benchmarks without relying on numerical velocity integration [^2609.10789]. Additionally, the LUCID framework integrates multi-view diffusion priors with projection-domain data consistency to recover missing Fourier components in laminographic X-ray nanoimaging of mammalian brain tissue [^2609.10456]. Spatially resolved atlases of pan-cancer brain metastases are being constructed using 10x Visium datasets, providing unprecedented single-cell and spatial context for tumor microenvironment analysis [^10.5281_zenodo.21816284][^10.5281_zenodo.21816285][^10.5281_zenodo.21782885][^10.5281_zenodo.21782884]. Research into metastatic mechanisms also highlights RET signaling as a key mediator in estrogen receptor-positive breast cancer brain metastasis [^10.5281_zenodo.19501083][^10.5281_zenodo.19501082].

## Computational Neuroscience and Brain Network Dynamics

Theoretical and computational models are increasingly bridging artificial intelligence with biological brain networks. The CoMA-DiT framework utilizes bidirectional cross-modal diffusion transformers to treat paired modalities as mutual generative supervision sources, significantly enhancing multimodal brain state decoding [^2609.11341]. For MEG data, a regression-based framework employing Wendland radial basis functions and model-based gradient boosting provides spatially resolved cortical activation estimates, overcoming the limitations of traditional cluster-based permutation tests [^2609.11278]. The "Platonic brain bridge hypothesis" proposes that omni models processing video, audio, and text converge on brain-like representations, with human cortical networks serving as architectural priors that boost benchmark accuracy [^2609.10947]. Directed cortical signal flow during motor execution has been mapped using Permutation Transfer Entropy, demonstrating that inter-hemispheric information dynamics are functionally conserved across right- and left-handed individuals [^2609.10870]. Foundation models for fMRI are being optimized through BrainTaskonomy, which organizes pretraining and adaptation via measured learning relations and directed taskonomy routing [^2609.10518]. Theoretical work on neural activity models shows that criticality emerges naturally in large-scale systems without fine-tuning, as parameter space regions near critical points map to large observable statistics [^2609.09438]. Scalable neuronal reconstruction is addressed by SkNeXt, a topology-first framework that converts morphology into compact skeletons to drastically reduce computational overhead in petabyte-scale microscopy [^2609.09832]. Olfactory system research reveals a spatial code governing receptor choice that aligns sensory maps between the nose and brain [^10.5281_zenodo.18941307][^10.5281_zenodo.18941308], while gut-brain axis studies identify cholecystokinin signaling in the nucleus of the solitary tract as a local regulator of satiety [^10.7273_000008411]. Comprehensive arteriovenous metabolomics have also quantified inter-organ metabolite exchange, revealing significant brain-specific uptake and release patterns [^10.5281_zenodo.20652506][^10.5281_zenodo.20652505].

## Brain Aging, Neurodegeneration, and Biomarkers

Longitudinal modeling and explainable AI are refining brain age estimation and neurodegenerative biomarker discovery. Brain-PACE extends Siamese longitudinal frameworks to directly estimate structural brain aging pace from paired T1-weighted MRIs, showing stronger associations with tau burden and cognitive impairment than indirect cross-sectional methods [^2609.11378]. Covariance neural networks (VNNs) operating on graph representations of covariance matrices provide refined stability bounds and transferability characterizations, offering principled alternatives to PCA-based pipelines for brain age gap analysis in neurodegeneration [^2609.10490]. The XAI-Refine framework introduces an automated explanation-knowledge loop that iteratively aligns brain-age prediction models with verified neurobiological literature through differentiable constraints [^2609.09388]. In Alzheimer's research, a critical evolutionary perspective argues against broad-spectrum amyloid precursor protein (APP) suppression, positioning APP as a universal pro-survival connector rather than merely a pathological substrate [^10.5281_zenodo.20049820]. Cross-cultural studies on category fluency highlight how age-related gray matter volume changes in the DLPFC and MTL interact with culturally shaped cognitive strategies [^10.48617_etd.1671]. Furthermore, circular RNAs (circRNAs) have emerged as stable biological age markers in the Drosophila brain, with accumulation rates correlating to biological rather than chronological age [^10.48617_etd.1568]. Predicting blood-brain barrier (BBB) permeability remains crucial for CNS drug development; explainable machine learning frameworks using molecular descriptors have achieved high accuracy in identifying key physicochemical drivers like TPSA and LogP [^2609.10012], while quantum reservoir computing architectures utilizing discrete time crystals show promise in forecasting BBB permeability and electronic gaps [^2609.11071].

## Glioblastoma and Targeted Therapeutics

Glioblastoma (GBM) research is increasingly leveraging liquid biopsies, epigenetic profiling, and advanced nanomedicine. Nucleosome positioning analysis in cell-free DNA (cfDNA) from GBM patients reveals reproducible family- and tissue-specific repositioning around transcription factor binding sites, offering non-invasive diagnostic potential [^10.5526_err-00042862]. Broader cfDNA repeat profiling establishes a comprehensive atlas of nucleosome signatures that successfully discriminate cancer patients from healthy controls across multiple tumor types, including GBM [^10.5526_err-00042900]. Therapeutic delivery across the blood-brain barrier is being advanced through lipid nanoparticle (LNP) platforms encapsulating siRNA targeting TERT, demonstrating robust gene silencing and cytotoxic effects in GBM cell lines [^10.17030_uclan.thesis.00059253]. Similarly, lipid-based nanostructured lipid carriers and lipid nanoparticles loaded with epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG) show promising colloidal stability and drug encapsulation rates for future GBM therapies [^10.17030_uclan.thesis.00059033]. At the cellular level, secretomes and extracellular vesicles (EVs) derived from neural crest stem cells significantly inhibit GBM proliferation and migration by modulating NF-κB activation and normalizing the anti-tumor immune microenvironment, highlighting their potential to overcome chemoresistance [^10.48683_1926.00128572].
