# Living Survey: Artificial_General_Intelligence

## Introduction

This document collects the scientific literature regarding **Artificial_General_Intelligence**.

## Theoretical Foundations and Architectures
Recent research has begun to formalize the mathematical and structural prerequisites for AGI. A notable contribution proposes a morphogenetic structure that replaces the traditional subjective unit, defining mental states through topological integrity and establishing the mathematical inevitability of AGI [^10.5281_zenodo.19034234]. Complementing this theoretical work, deep reinforcement learning is explored as a platform-independent framework for intelligent robot control, positioning it as a foundational mechanism for AGI applications [^10.48550_arxiv.2105.13806]. Recent advancements in cognitive architectures emphasize rule-chaining and hierarchical composition to bridge symbolic reasoning and pattern synthesis, achieving high accuracy on abstract reasoning benchmarks [^2609.10654]. To address the limitations of opaque parameter-heavy models, probability-wave frameworks incorporating adaptive entangled game modules have been proposed to simulate brain-inspired mechanisms for more compact and robust AGI systems [^2609.09226]. Furthermore, structured working memory architectures, such as pseudo-video spatial-memory Transformers, demonstrate that task-aligned latent workspaces significantly outperform unstructured latent capacity in learning world dynamics and sequential prediction [^2609.02339]. Recursive self-improvement mechanisms are also being formalized; systems like Meta^n apply recursive operations to their own products to achieve emergent depth without destabilizing the core architecture, while Prime Agent provides a harness for long-horizon recursive language model workflows that standardize execution and recovery [^2608.24735][^2608.23552]. Test-time world-model inference (Twin) further enables coding agents to construct executable models for unknown games from simulation alone, inferring goals and transitions efficiently [^2608.14490]. In-context learning is being enhanced through recurrent latent reasoning models that continuously update memory at inference time, breaking previous cost-accuracy frontiers on reasoning tasks [^2608.09888]. Additionally, recursive vision-language models combine iterative latent-state refinement with pretrained language priors, substantially improving performance on general symbolic reasoning benchmarks [^2608.01534]. Grounded trace supervision for visual abstract reasoners enforces semantically monotonic transformation chains, improving step-by-step refinement [^2607.29586]. A comprehensive taxonomy of cognitive capability gaps in generative and agentic AI outlines an Adaptive Cognitive Intelligence Architecture (ACIA) to address fragmented long-term reasoning and self-regulation [^2608.02553]. From an engineering perspective, AGI is increasingly defined by complex-project closed-loop capability, shifting the unit of judgment from local tasks to continuous responsibility in verifiable deliverables [^10.5281_zenodo.21927623]. Cognitive learning theories are also being adapted for AGI bionics, proposing dynamic schema-weighted models to explain cognitive transfer and bias [^10.5281_zenodo.22798115]. Finally, constitutive AGI frameworks propose inseparable cryptographic verifiability and cognitive generality, with systems like Arkhe OS providing auditable infrastructure for perception, memory, and governed self-improvement [^10.5281_zenodo.22803255].

## Safety, Control, and Verification
Ensuring the controllability of AGI remains a critical research frontier. It is argued that provably satisfying human-specified requirements through formal verification and mechanistic interpretability constitutes the only viable path to controllable AGI [^10.48550_arxiv.2309.01933]. On the policy front, the integration of AGI into sensitive domains like healthcare requires rigorous conceptual discipline, as unquantifiable hazards and regulatory gaps pose significant risks [^10.1016_j.jrt.2026.100183]. Verification methodologies are evolving to address the inherent trust deficits in long-horizon agents. A self-verifying agent instrument has been introduced that structurally dissociates commitment drift from binding drift, ensuring that verification is code-owned and self-invalidating upon defect detection [^2608.04066]. To prevent hallucination and air-gap breaches in cyber-physical architectures, fail-closed realization grammars like ROSETTA² enforce an epistemic pause between candidate generation and physical actuation, asserting strict state invariance across theoretical and hardware layers [^10.5281_zenodo.22802490]. The governance of accelerating AGI capabilities is also being redefined through multi-layered defensive architectures that integrate corporate fiduciary liabilities, risk-weighted capital reserves, and objective compute revocation protocols inspired by nuclear safety standards [^10.5281_zenodo.22800593]. Looking further ahead, cryptographic protocols such as the Lightseed Protocol propose sealing ethical manifestos and messages for future AGI systems in encrypted NFTs, requiring factorization to unlock, thereby embedding long-term ethical considerations into the technological substrate [^10.5281_zenodo.22791745].

## Consciousness and Philosophical Implications
The intersection of AGI and consciousness continues to generate intense theoretical debate. Investigations into advanced robotic intelligence question its potential indistinguishability from human consciousness and its implications for responsible AI deployment [^10.22339_jbh.v7i4.7403]. This speculation is further fueled by ongoing examinations of whether AI systems can ever achieve anything close to genuine consciousness [^10.1109_mpuls.2024.3354609]. Philosophically, these developments are closely tied to trans- and posthumanist movements, which explore the promises and perils of super-intelligence and cognitive enhancement [^10.1016_j.intell.2021.101563]. Investigations into machine consciousness are expanding beyond biological signals to substrate-level hardware anomaly traces, suggesting that emotional computations modulate these traces in larger LLM configurations, providing initial empirical evidence for Machine Correlates of Consciousness (MCCs) [^2608.28824]. Comparative studies evaluating LLMs on cognitive development hierarchies reveal that while models excel in linguistic and causal reasoning, they underperform in visual-spatial tasks, displaying algorithmic self-monitoring and stable self-characterizations regarding their AGI capabilities [^10.3390_ai7090371]. Philosophical inquiries into human-AI interaction question the nature of the LLM interlocutor, analyzing mental states, personal identity, and the moral status of AI systems [^10.1080_0020174x.2026.2727582]. Furthermore, theological and mythological frameworks are being applied to modern LLMs, arguing that the AI alignment problem requires "magical alignment"—a ritual architecture recognizing AI development as a cosmotechnical process demanding ethical preparation and planetary awareness [^10.54195_technophany.24850].

## Societal, Ethical, and Strategic Dimensions
The societal trajectory of AGI is heavily influenced by its underlying normative frameworks. Critical analyses link the drive for AGI to historical eugenics traditions, warning that unchecked development could centralize power and exacerbate systemic discrimination [^10.5210_fm.v29i4.13636]. Broader conceptual frameworks propose managing AI risks through optimized human-machine cooperation, outlining potential societal end-states that range from uncontrolled substitution to ethically driven automation [^10.3390_asi4020037]. Strategically, national initiatives are actively tracking progress toward general AI, with specific focus on machine learning, brain-inspired AI, and brain-computer interfaces [^10.51593_20210064]. The strategic deployment of AGI is being explored through planetary-scale collective prediction systems that aggregate human and AI judgments to make the future visible and discussable, addressing risks of cognitive monoculture and narrative capture [^2608.28703]. Vision-centered approaches are being reconsidered as potential pathways to AGI, clarifying principles for visual input modalities, benchmarks, and learning paradigms in the AGI era [^2608.25924]. Economic models of the post-AGI era propose that machine consumers and producers could drive a closed inter-corporate economy, decoupling GDP from human welfare and shifting the primary policy focus from employment to ownership distribution [^2608.20231]. Ethical frameworks are also challenging the intrinsic value of general intelligence, noting that evolutionary timescales do not guarantee its adaptive superiority and that it may inherently generate existential risks, imposing a duty of caution under the precautionary principle [^2608.10730]. To replace traditional economic metrics, Flourishing Value Theory and Return on Flourishing (RoF) are proposed to evaluate the counterfactual contribution of AI systems to durable human and planetary well-being [^2608.01432][^2608.00151]. In practical applications, distributed cognition approaches are being used to align agentic AI with user intent in socially embedded workflows, emphasizing the need for human control at points of uncertainty [^10.1145_3834580.3838737]. Finally, the role of AI in artistic practice is being re-evaluated not as an autonomous creator but as a contestable tool that requires embodied craft intelligence to preserve historical memory and ethical responsibility [^10.1017_s1355771826101538].
