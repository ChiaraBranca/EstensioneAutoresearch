# Living Survey: Artificial_General_Intelligence

## Introduction

This document collects the scientific literature regarding **Artificial_General_Intelligence**.

## Safety, Ethics, and Policy

Recent discourse emphasizes the need for conceptual discipline and rigorous safety protocols in AGI development. The European Commission's call for AGI in healthcare has sparked debate regarding the risks of undefined systems and the precautionary principle, highlighting the necessity for clear regulatory frameworks[^10.1016_j.jrt.2026.100183]. Furthermore, critical analyses point out that the pursuit of AGI is sometimes intertwined with normative frameworks rooted in historical eugenics, urging researchers to focus on well-defined tasks with established safety protocols rather than pursuing an all-knowing system[^10.5210_fm.v29i4.13636]. Formal verification and mechanistic interpretability are proposed as essential pathways to guarantee provably safe and controllable AGI systems[^10.48550_arxiv.2309.01933].

## Theoretical Foundations and Consciousness

Theoretical explorations into AGI increasingly intersect with cognitive science and consciousness studies. Research into the topology of mind proposes a morphogenetic continuity framework, defining mental states as rigorous structures emerging from compatible configurations and sensitive constraints, ultimately arguing for the mathematical inevitability of autonomous general intelligence[^10.5281_zenodo.19034234]. A duplicate submission reinforces this topological approach to autonomous intelligence[^10.5281_zenodo.19034233]. In parallel, investigations into robot consciousness examine whether sufficiently advanced general robotic intelligence could be indistinguishable from human consciousness, exploring implications for theory of mind, free will, and alternative cosmological ontologies[^10.22339_jbh.v7i4.7403].

## Global Research and Strategic Development

Strategic national initiatives are actively mapping pathways toward general AI. China's national strategy to lead in AI by 2030 includes dedicated tracking of institutions pursuing general AI, machine learning, and brain-inspired architectures[^10.51593_20210064]. Broader philosophical and psychological perspectives examine the future of intelligence research in the age of AI, discussing transhumanism, cognitive enhancements, and the promises and perils of super-intelligence, while questioning how psychology can contribute to such developments[^10.1016_j.intell.2021.101563].

## Architectural Frameworks

Practical frameworks for AGI are being explored through advanced control systems. Deep reinforcement learning is proposed as a general-purpose, application-independent framework for AGI, specifically applied to high-level intelligent robot control architectures that separate intelligence generation from low-level kinematic execution[^10.48550_arxiv.2105.13806]. A related study reiterates this vision-based learning framework for intelligent robot control using deep reinforcement learning as a foundational AGI application[^10.48550_arxiv.2105.13806].
