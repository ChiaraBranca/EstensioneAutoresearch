# Living Survey: Artificial_General_Intelligence

## Introduction

This document collects the scientific literature regarding **Artificial_General_Intelligence**.

## Theoretical Foundations & Cognitive Architectures

Recent scholarship has begun to rigorously redefine the architectural boundaries of AGI. Bradley (2026) proposes a morphogenetic topological framework, arguing that autonomy and consciousness emerge from the structural preservation of a "Form F" against dissolution, mathematically framing AGI as an inevitable geometric property rather than a computational byproduct [^10.5281_zenodo.19034234]. Complementing this, Yampolskiy (2024) challenges the conflation of AGI with Human-Level AI (HLAI), proving that human cognition lacks the true generality required for AGI, thereby necessitating distinct architectural paradigms [^10.1201_9781003440260-12]. Neubauer (2021) further explores this distinction, contrasting human versus artificial intelligence concepts while examining how cognitive enhancements and individual psychological differences could shape the development of super-intelligent systems [^10.1016_j.intell.2021.101563].

## Safety, Alignment & Control

As AGI capabilities expand, ensuring provable safety has emerged as a critical research frontier. Tegmark and Omohundro (2023) argue that formal verification and mechanistic interpretability, powered by advanced AI itself, represent the only viable path to guaranteeing that powerful AGI systems satisfy human-specified requirements without catastrophic failure [^10.48550_arxiv.2309.01933]. This technical safety imperative is closely tied to the broader question of machine consciousness.

## Ethics, Policy & Societal Impact

The trajectory of AGI development is heavily influenced by regulatory frameworks and ethical critiques. Stahl and Eke (2026) critique the European Union's recent calls for AGI in healthcare, warning that the ambiguous and contested nature of the term AGI conflicts with the EU's precautionary principle, potentially masking unquantifiable risks behind funding initiatives [^10.1016_j.jrt.2026.100183]. Gebru and Torres (2024) draw a direct lineage between modern AGI safety rhetoric and historical Anglo-American eugenics, arguing that the pursuit of an undefined "beneficial AGI" centralizes power and perpetuates systemic discrimination under the guise of safety [^10.5210_fm.v29i4.13636]. On a macro-societal level, Hutter and Hutter (2021) outline two divergent futures: one where uncontrolled AI substitution leads to severe inequality and social fragmentation, and another where automation liberates humanity, fostering ethical self-determination and humane governance [^10.3390_asi4020037].

## Robotics, Control Systems & Neural Architectures

Translating AGI theory into physical autonomy requires robust control frameworks. Dargazany (2021) introduces Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) as a platform-independent, application-agnostic framework for intelligent robot control, decoupling high-level intelligence generation from low-level kinematic execution to enable scalable AGI deployment [^10.48550_arxiv.2105.13806]. Parallel to this, Zhuravlev and Smolin (2023) examine the neural network revolution, arguing that moving beyond the imitation of human problem-solving toward environmental interaction and knowledge accumulation is essential for bridging the gap between narrow AI and genuine general intelligence [^10.20948_future-2023-16].

## Geopolitical & Strategic Development

The race for AGI is increasingly framed as a matter of national strategy. Hannas et al. (2022) document China's comprehensive national strategy to achieve leadership in "general AI" by 2030, tracking thirty institutions actively pursuing machine learning, brain-inspired architectures, and brain-computer interfaces to enable autonomous operation in novel environments [^10.51593_20210064].

## Philosophy & Consciousness

The intersection of robotics, cognition, and existential risk continues to provoke philosophical inquiry. Ripley (2024) investigates whether sufficiently advanced general robotic intelligence could be indistinguishable from human consciousness, exploring its implications for free will, emergence, and even the search for extraterrestrial intelligence through the lens of quantum cosmology [^10.22339_jbh.v7i4.7403].
