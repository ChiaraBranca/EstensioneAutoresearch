# Living Survey: Artificial_General_Intelligence

## Introduction

This document collects the scientific literature regarding **Artificial_General_Intelligence**.

## Theoretical Foundations & Cognitive Architectures

Recent scholarship has begun to rigorously redefine the architectural boundaries of AGI. Bradley (2026) proposes a morphogenetic topological framework, arguing that autonomy and consciousness emerge from the structural preservation of a "Form F" against dissolution, mathematically framing AGI as an inevitable geometric property rather than a computational byproduct [^10.5281_zenodo.19034234]. Complementing this, Yampolskiy (2024) challenges the conflation of AGI with Human-Level AI (HLAI), proving that human cognition lacks the true generality required for AGI, thereby necessitating distinct architectural paradigms [^10.1201_9781003440260-12]. Neubauer (2021) further explores this distinction, contrasting human versus artificial intelligence concepts while examining how cognitive enhancements and individual psychological differences could shape the development of super-intelligent systems [^10.1016_j.intell.2021.101563]. 

Recent advancements in cognitive architectures emphasize exact representational criteria and hierarchical rule induction. Marinho Rocha et al. (2026) introduce "exactness at inference" as a necessary condition for out-of-distribution generalization, demonstrating that models must compute structurally equivalent representations rather than approximations to succeed on benchmarks like ARC-AGI [^2609.24942]. Complementing this, Kar (2026) presents a multi-stage rule-chaining framework that integrates deterministic rule discovery, pattern-composition engines, and structural abstraction layers to achieve over 95% accuracy on ARC-AGI-2 tasks through interpretable compositional reasoning [^2609.10654]. Furthermore, Deliège et al. (2026) propose a two-step test-time training protocol (Embed-TTT) that isolates task embeddings from backbone finetuning, enabling precise linear probing of underlying geometric rules and significantly improving performance on ARC-AGI-1 and ConceptARC [^2609.21181].

## Safety, Alignment & Control

As AGI capabilities expand, ensuring provable safety has emerged as a critical research frontier. Tegmark and Omohundro (2023) argue that formal verification and mechanistic interpretability, powered by advanced AI itself, represent the only viable path to guaranteeing that powerful AGI systems satisfy human-specified requirements without catastrophic failure [^10.48550_arxiv.2309.01933]. This technical safety imperative is closely tied to the broader question of machine consciousness.

Long-term safety strategies are increasingly shifting toward collective prediction and systemic governance. Zhang et al. (2026) propose the "Save 2050" initiative, a distributed planetary-scale prediction system that aggregates human and AI judgments to simulate and inspect "predicted worlds," aiming to make future trajectories visible and discussable to mitigate singularity risks and cognitive monoculture [^2608.28703].

## Ethics, Policy & Societal Impact

The trajectory of AGI development is heavily influenced by regulatory frameworks and ethical critiques. Stahl and Eke (2026) critique the European Union's recent calls for AGI in healthcare, warning that the ambiguous and contested nature of the term AGI conflicts with the EU's precautionary principle, potentially masking unquantifiable risks behind funding initiatives [^10.1016_j.jrt.2026.100183]. Gebru and Torres (2024) draw a direct lineage between modern AGI safety rhetoric and historical Anglo-American eugenics, arguing that the pursuit of an undefined "beneficial AGI" centralizes power and perpetuates systemic discrimination under the guise of safety [^10.5210_fm.v29i4.13636]. On a macro-societal level, Hutter and Hutter (2021) outline two divergent futures: one where uncontrolled AI substitution leads to severe inequality and social fragmentation, and another where automation liberates humanity, fostering ethical self-determination and humane governance [^10.3390_asi4020037].

The economic and evolutionary implications of AGI are undergoing rigorous re-evaluation. Sharma (2026) models a post-AGI economy where machine consumers and producers drive a closed inter-corporate system, demonstrating that GDP can completely decouple from human welfare, rendering traditional employment policies obsolete and elevating ownership distribution to the sole determinant of human survival [^2608.20231]. Concurrently, Klotz (2026) mounts an evolutionary challenge to the intrinsic value of general intelligence, arguing that cognitive sophistication may generate unique existential liabilities and that the uncertainty surrounding AGI's long-term adaptive value imposes a strict ethical duty of caution under the precautionary principle [^2608.10730].

## Robotics, Control Systems & Neural Architectures

Translating AGI theory into physical autonomy requires robust control frameworks. Dargazany (2021) introduces Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) as a platform-independent, application-agnostic framework for intelligent robot control, decoupling high-level intelligence generation from low-level kinematic execution to enable scalable AGI deployment [^10.48550_arxiv.2105.13806]. Parallel to this, Zhuravlev and Smolin (2023) examine the neural network revolution, arguing that moving beyond the imitation of human problem-solving toward environmental interaction and knowledge accumulation is essential for bridging the gap between narrow AI and genuine general intelligence [^10.20948_future-2023-16].

Novel neural architectures are being designed to handle complex reasoning and adaptive behaviors. Suleymanzade et al. (2026) introduce "looped flows," which train recurrence via local denoising objectives to enable models to solve harder problems by allocating more inference-time computation, achieving state-of-the-art results on ARC-AGI-1 and ARC-AGI-2 [^2609.11801]. In a different paradigm, Li et al. (2026) propose a probability-wave framework for AGI that models adaptive entangled game modules, drawing parallels to nonlocal brain mechanisms and suggesting that integrating ANN-based AI with entangled-brain simulations could yield more compact and robust human-like processing units [^2609.09226].

## Geopolitical & Strategic Development

The race for AGI is increasingly framed as a matter of national strategy. Hannas et al. (2022) document China's comprehensive national strategy to achieve leadership in "general AI" by 2030, tracking thirty institutions actively pursuing machine learning, brain-inspired architectures, and brain-computer interfaces to enable autonomous operation in novel environments [^10.51593_20210064].

## Philosophy & Consciousness

The intersection of robotics, cognition, and existential risk continues to provoke philosophical inquiry. Ripley (2024) investigates whether sufficiently advanced general robotic intelligence could be indistinguishable from human consciousness, exploring its implications for free will, emergence, and even the search for extraterrestrial intelligence through the lens of quantum cosmology [^10.22339_jbh.v7i4.7403].

## Benchmarking & Evaluation

Evaluating AGI capabilities requires moving beyond static input-output pairs toward dynamic, multi-dimensional benchmarks. Beger et al. (2026) introduce PotARCin, a benchmark that assesses abstract rule understanding across five dimensions (Definition, Classification, Constrained Generation, Editing, and Inversion), revealing a massive performance gap between standard ARC evaluation and holistic rule comprehension [^2609.27288]. Similarly, Potapov (2026) presents the AGI Maze Prediction Datasets, a controlled testbed for studying world dynamics in Transformers, demonstrating that structured, task-aligned working memory significantly outperforms unstructured latent capacity in learning transferable action-conditioned dynamics [^2609.02339].

## Multi-Agent & Socio-Affective Systems

The shift toward collaborative and socially-aware AGI architectures is gaining traction. Berga (2026) outlines the AGIMUD software architecture, which integrates socially-aware reasoning, emotion modeling, and distributed processing to enable real-time human-agent interactions in simulated dynamic worlds [^2609.26927]. Park et al. (2026) demonstrate that test-time communication among multi-agent teams substantially outperforms independent parallel attempts on complex reasoning tasks like ARC-AGI-3, showing that sharing breakthroughs allows teams to solve tasks that no single agent could achieve alone [^2609.21032].

## In-Context Learning & World Modeling

Leveraging in-context observations for world modeling has proven highly effective for AGI reasoning. Matsutani et al. (2026) show that few-shot demonstrations from diverse worlds relocate and enhance predictive world representations in LLMs, significantly boosting performance on ARC-AGI-1&2 and web agent tasks [^2609.24352]. Engdahl et al. (2026) introduce BDH-CQ, a reasoning model combining in-context learning with recurrent latent reasoning, which continuously updates its memory at inference time to achieve a new cost-accuracy Pareto frontier on ARC-AGI-1 [^2608.09888].

## Self-Improving & Recursive Systems

Recursive self-improvement and persistent execution harnesses are pushing the boundaries of autonomous AGI agents. Kim et al. (2026) present Meta^n, a system that recursively applies a fixed meta-operation to its own outputs, achieving emergent depth and outperforming prior self-improving agents on ARC-AGI-2 without destabilizing the editing machinery [^2608.24735]. Complementing this, Karten et al. (2026) introduce Prime Agent, an open-source harness for long-horizon recursive language model workflows that preserves memories and skills across trajectories, raising ARC-AGI-3 performance from 30% to 95.5% through continuous refinement and subagent coordination [^2608.23552].

## Mechanistic Interpretability

Understanding the internal mechanics of reasoning models is critical for reliable AGI. Rodrigues and Kang (2026) conduct a mechanistic study of Hierarchical Reasoning Models (HRM) on ARC-AGI-2, revealing that recurrent states implement constraint-aware iterative refinement rather than relying on compact, causally important feature sets, highlighting the need for interpretability techniques tailored to latent-space recursive reasoning [^2608.28824].

## Multimodal & Visual AGI

Vision is increasingly recognized as a potential primary pathway to AGI. Kataoka et al. (2026) publish a white paper on Visual General Intelligence (VGI), arguing that intelligence emerging from visual experience and learning may provide a more robust foundation than language alone, and outlining core principles, benchmarks, and learning paradigms for vision-centered AGI development [^2608.25924].

## World Modeling & Simulation

Constructing executable world models from interaction alone represents a major leap in autonomous reasoning. Skoutnev et al. (2026) introduce Twin, a system where a coding agent writes an executable world model to complete continual learning tasks like ARC-AGI-3 games, achieving 97.8% level clearance by inferring game rules and goals through simulation and replay validation [^2608.14490].

## Hybrid Reasoning Architectures

Combining symbolic search with neural generation offers promising results for complex reasoning. Dyachenko (2026) demonstrates that seeding genetic algorithms with programs generated by a quantized LLM dramatically improves success rates on ARC-AGI-2, solving 10% of tasks compared to 3.3% with LLMs alone, proving that genetic search can effectively refine neural outputs [^2609.27242].
