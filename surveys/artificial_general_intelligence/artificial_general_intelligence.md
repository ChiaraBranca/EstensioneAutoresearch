# Living Survey: Artificial_General_Intelligence

## Introduction

This document collects the scientific literature regarding **Artificial_General_Intelligence**.

## Policy and Ethical Implications

Recent policy discussions in the European Union have highlighted the need for conceptual discipline when addressing Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) in healthcare. The authors argue that the term AGI is contested and potentially risky, suggesting that its use in funding calls may conflict with established precautionary principles. This underscores the growing tension between rapid technological advancement and regulatory frameworks [^10.1016_j.jrt.2026.100183]. 

Critical analysis of the AGI development movement reveals deep normative roots in the Anglo-American eugenics tradition. The authors argue that framing AGI as an unquestioned goal for "safety" and "benefiting humanity" masks discriminatory attitudes that could centralize power and harm marginalized groups. This perspective urges a shift toward developing defined, testable systems rather than pursuing an all-knowing AGI [^10.5210_fm.v29i4.13636].

## Cognitive Architectures and Consciousness

This work proposes a foundational shift in cognitive science by replacing the subjective unit with a "Form F" to describe the transition from computation to autonomy. It frames consciousness as the systemic capacity to integrate stochastic perturbations into a stable gradient, mathematically linking autonomy and survival instincts to the preservation of topological integrity. This approach establishes a rigorous geometric framework for understanding the emergence of AGI [^10.5281_zenodo.19034234].

The intersection of logic, metamathematics, and brain sciences has renewed interest in artificial consciousness. This paper explores conjectures regarding sufficiently advanced general robotic intelligence, questioning whether it would be indistinguishable from human consciousness. It further examines the implications of such systems in responsible roles and their relationship to broader theories of mind, emergence, and free will [^10.22339_jbh.v7i4.7403].

## Safety and Verification

This paper outlines a pathway to safely deploying powerful AGIs by ensuring they provably satisfy human-specified requirements. It argues that advanced AI for formal verification and mechanistic interpretability will soon make this technically feasible, presenting it as the only guaranteed path to controllable AGI. The authors propose a set of challenge problems to advance this critical safety research [^10.48550_arxiv.2309.01933].

## Geopolitical Strategies

China's national strategy explicitly targets leadership in "general AI" capable of autonomous action in novel circumstances by 2030. Open-source research identifies numerous institutions engaged in machine learning, brain-inspired AI, and brain-computer interfaces. This report highlights the geopolitical race to achieve AGI and the mechanisms in place to track progress and provide timely alerts [^10.51593_20210064].

## Philosophical Perspectives

This contribution examines the intersection of human intelligence research, cognitive enhancements, and the philosophical movements of trans- and posthumanism. It discusses the promises and perils of developing an artificial "super-intelligence," outlining how psychology and individual differences should inform the development of such systems to ensure they align with human values and societal needs [^10.1016_j.intell.2021.101563].
