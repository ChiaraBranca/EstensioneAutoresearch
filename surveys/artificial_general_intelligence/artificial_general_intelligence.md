# Living Survey: Artificial_General_Intelligence

## Introduction

This document collects the scientific literature regarding **Artificial_General_Intelligence**.

## Recent Developments and Theoretical Frameworks

Recent scholarship continues to explore the architectural, ethical, and policy dimensions of AGI. Theoretical work has proposed novel mathematical frameworks for autonomous intelligence, suggesting that consciousness and autonomy may emerge from morphogenetic structures and topological integrity rather than traditional computational models of the subject [^10.5281_zenodo.19034234]. Concurrently, the pursuit of AGI has sparked critical debates regarding its safety and societal implications. Researchers have highlighted the risks of framing AGI as an unquestioned goal, arguing that undefined systems like AGI cannot be adequately tested for safety and that the drive toward it may perpetuate discriminatory historical paradigms [^10.5210_fm.v29i4.13636]. 

In parallel, significant efforts are directed toward ensuring controllable and provably safe AGI systems. Formal verification and mechanistic interpretability are proposed as essential pathways to guarantee that powerful AGIs satisfy human-specified requirements, thereby mitigating existential risks [^10.48550_arxiv.2309.01933]. The intersection of AGI and robotics also raises profound questions about consciousness, with conjectures exploring whether sufficiently advanced general robotic intelligence could become indistinguishable from human consciousness or even serve as a substitute in critical roles [^10.1109_mpuls.2024.3354609][^10.22339_jbh.v7i4.7403]. 

From a policy and strategic perspective, the conceptualization of AGI remains contested. The European Union's recent calls for AGI in healthcare have been critiqued for lacking conceptual discipline, potentially conflating advanced narrow AI with qualitatively novel, unpredictable technologies that warrant a precautionary approach [^10.1016_j.jrt.2026.100183]. Globally, national strategies are actively tracking progress toward general AI, with specific focus on machine learning, brain-inspired AI, and brain-computer interfaces as key pillars for achieving autonomous capabilities [^10.51593_20210064]. 

Methodologically, deep reinforcement learning is increasingly viewed as a general-purpose framework for AGI, offering application-independent and platform-independent control architectures that incrementally improve intelligence through environmental interaction [^10.48550_arxiv.2105.13806]. Furthermore, the transition from narrow AI to more generalized capabilities requires abandoning mere imitation of human problem-solving in favor of systems that accumulate new knowledge through direct environmental interaction [^10.20948_future-2023-16]. The broader implications of super-intelligence and human-AI synergy continue to shape psychological and philosophical research, emphasizing the need to understand individual cognitive differences in the development of future intelligent systems [^10.1016_j.intell.2021.101563].
