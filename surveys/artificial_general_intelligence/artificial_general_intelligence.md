# Living Survey: Artificial_General_Intelligence

## Introduction

This document collects the scientific literature regarding **Artificial_General_Intelligence**.

## Theoretical Architectures and Mechanisms

Recent research continues to explore the foundational mathematical and cognitive architectures required for autonomous general intelligence. Bradley (2026) proposes a morphogenetic framework where the "Form F" replaces the traditional subjective unit, arguing that consciousness and autonomy emerge from topological integrity and structural tension rather than qualitative residues[^10.5281_zenodo.19034234]. This geometric approach to mind suggests that AGI is a mathematical inevitability given the right structural constraints.

On a geopolitical and strategic level, Hannas et al. (2022) highlight China's national strategy to achieve leadership in general AI by 2030, tracking 30 institutions focusing on machine learning, brain-inspired AI, and brain-computer interfaces[^10.51593_20210064]. This underscores the global race to develop autonomous systems capable of novel, unstructured problem-solving.

## Safety, Ethics, and Societal Implications

As AGI capabilities advance, the discourse around safety, conceptual discipline, and ethical frameworks has intensified. Stahl and Eke (2026) critique the European Commission's recent calls for AGI in healthcare, arguing that the term "AGI" is fundamentally problematic due to its contested nature and potential for unpredictable risks. They advocate for conceptual discipline and adherence to the precautionary principle in EU AI policy[^10.1016_j.jrt.2026.100183].

Gebru and Torres (2024) challenge the normative framework driving AGI development, linking it to historical eugenicist traditions. They argue that undefined systems like "AGI" cannot be safely tested and that the pursuit of a supposedly all-knowing system centralizes power while harming marginalized groups, urging a shift toward defined, safety-protocol-driven tasks[^10.5210_fm.v29i4.13636].

Tegmark and Omohundro (2023) propose a technical pathway to controllable AGI through provably safe systems, leveraging advanced AI for formal verification and mechanistic interpretability to guarantee alignment with human-specified requirements[^10.48550_arxiv.2309.01933].

## Consciousness and Advanced Robotics

The intersection of AGI, robotics, and consciousness remains a critical frontier. Ripley (2024) explores whether sufficiently advanced general robotic intelligence could be indistinguishable from human consciousness, examining implications for ethics, free will, and even extraterrestrial intelligence analogues[^10.22339_jbh.v7i4.7403]. Johnson (2023) further contextualizes this within the broader scientific speculation on whether AI systems can ever achieve genuine consciousness, highlighting the intense debate surrounding the theoretical and practical boundaries of machine minds[^10.1109_mpuls.2024.3354609].
