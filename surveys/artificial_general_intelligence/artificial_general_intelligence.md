# Living Survey: Artificial_General_Intelligence

## Introduction

## Theoretical Architectures and Mechanisms

Recent research continues to explore the foundational mathematical and cognitive architectures required for autonomous general intelligence. Bradley (2026) proposes a morphogenetic framework where the "Form F" replaces the traditional subjective unit, arguing that consciousness and autonomy emerge from topological integrity and structural tension rather than qualitative residues[^10.5281_zenodo.19034234]. This geometric approach to mind suggests that AGI is a mathematical inevitability given the right structural constraints.

On a geopolitical and strategic level, Hannas et al. (2022) highlight China's national strategy to achieve leadership in general AI by 2030, tracking 30 institutions focusing on machine learning, brain-inspired AI, and brain-computer interfaces[^10.51593_20210064]. This underscores the global race to develop autonomous systems capable of novel, unstructured problem-solving.

Li et al. (2026) introduce adaptive entangled game modules based on a probability-wave framework, arguing that AGI architectures must incorporate nonlocal entangled mechanisms to capture collective adaptive behaviors beyond opaque ANN parameters[^2609.09226]. Kataoka et al. (2026) publish a white paper on Visual General Intelligence (VGI), arguing that intelligence emerging from visual experience and learning provides a viable pathway toward AGI, outlining necessary principles, modalities, and benchmarks[^2608.25924]. Akinrele et al. (2026) present a taxonomy of cognitive capability gaps in generative and agentic AI, organizing literature around persistent state modeling, goal-directed autonomy, and self-monitoring to guide the design of cognitively capable systems[^2608.02553]. Kanth (2026) formalizes a three-timescale architecture for persistent memory and recursive online learning, distinguishing inference-time state adaptation from online parameter learning and proposing controlled experiments for consolidation and plasticity gating[^10.5281_zenodo.22842040]. Sawa (2026) presents the Logic of Existence (LoE) handbook, developing a relational AI organizational model that connects sources, symbolic grammar, and relational rotors into a reproducible research workflow for AGI architectures[^10.5281_zenodo.22862826].

Bakshi (2026) argues that general intelligence requires structural constraints across multiple non-reducible levels of description, asserting that scaling alone cannot yield AGI without addressing these distinct architectural layers[^2607.18943]. Overmann (2026) introduces a computational theory of mind based on set theory and hyperdimensional computing, proposing that sparse binary data and subset pattern matching serve as the fundamental engine of cognition, bridging perceptual data and symbols without continuous bottlenecks[^2606.31819]. Majkic (2026) expands neuro-symbolic AGI architectures by integrating probabilistic computation into Belnap's Typed Intensional FOL, enabling neural networks to compute probability densities for unknown sentences while preserving logical deduction[^2607.13073]. Mugica Negueruela (2026) presents Oscurus, a dual-process neuro-symbolic architecture that leverages hyperdimensional computing for zero-token program synthesis, invoking generative models only when epistemic uncertainty exceeds a certainty threshold[^10.5281_zenodo.22848850].

Ushakov and Berdinsk (2026) propose a closed reentry loop architecture that mathematically guarantees self-model emergence and unprogrammed goal-directed behavior, positioning it as a topologically protected, safe-by-design approach to AGI[^2606.26406]. ## Safety, Ethics, and Societal Implications

As AGI capabilities advance, the discourse around safety, conceptual discipline, and ethical frameworks has intensified. Stahl and Eke (2026) critique the European Commission's recent calls for AGI in healthcare, arguing that the term "AGI" is fundamentally problematic due to its contested nature and potential for unpredictable risks. They advocate for conceptual discipline and adherence to the precautionary principle in EU AI policy[^10.1016_j.jrt.2026.100183].

Gebru and Torres (2024) challenge the normative framework driving AGI development, linking it to historical eugenicist traditions. They argue that undefined systems like "AGI" cannot be safely tested and that the pursuit of a supposedly all-knowing system centralizes power while harming marginalized groups, urging a shift toward defined, safety-protocol-driven tasks[^10.5210_fm.v29i4.13636].

Tegmark and Omohundro (2023) propose a technical pathway to controllable AGI through provably safe systems, leveraging advanced AI for formal verification and mechanistic interpretability to guarantee alignment with human-specified requirements[^10.48550_arxiv.2309.01933].

The trajectory toward AGI necessitates rigorous ethical and economic frameworks. Ruan et al. (2026) propose Return on Flourishing (RoF) as a primary success criterion for AI and post-AGI economic systems, extending ROI to evaluate counterfactual contributions to human and planetary well-being across physical, emotional, and relational dimensions[^2608.00151]. Cochinescu (2026) hypothesizes that perceived AGI believability stems from dimensional completeness—expressing first-person stances across time, truth, entropy, and love—rather than raw task capability, separating perceived mind from intelligence[^2607.15883]. Van Belle (2026) inverts AI ethics by examining how future sentient ASI might morally evaluate humanity, suggesting that technical design choices and humanity's moral behavior will shape our long-term standing in a post-ASI world[^2607.14998]. Maclean (2026) argues for Constitutive Co-Evolution (CCE), positing that humans and AI are structurally dependent, with human relationships providing grounded values and external error correction that advanced AI cannot generate internally[^10.5281_zenodo.22843331]. Li (2026) models post-AGI labor substitution as a structural phase transition, showing that even perfectly aligned AGI can drive societal obsolescence through synergistic functions and hysteresis, independent of alignment failures[^10.5281_zenodo.22843902]. Li (2026) further argues that traditional safety institutions rely on passivity and shared substrates that AGI violates, necessitating verifiable capability measurement and capability-permission separation[^10.5281_zenodo.22844023].

The mathematical and conceptual limits of AGI safety continue to be rigorously defined. Mezquita (2026) establishes the mathematical limits of AGI safety, proving that verifying a fixed system or a self-modifying system's safety is structurally impossible for highly expressive AGI, forcing a Soundness-Completeness-Tractability Trilemma[^2606.28639]. Briones (2026) develops DAF-AGI, a second-order conceptual artifact that treats AGI under-specification as a design and governance problem, proposing definitional sovereignty as an enabling component of algorithmic sovereignty[^2606.12713]. Maclean (2026) argues that caring is a biological achievement produced by neurochemical architecture, introducing the Relational Medium as a continuous flow of live outcome feedback necessary for AI values to remain functional rather than frozen[^10.5281_zenodo.22843449]. Li (2026) argues that traditional safety institutions rely on passivity and shared substrates that AGI violates, necessitating verifiable capability measurement and capability-permission separation[^10.5281_zenodo.22844022]. Turner (2026) presents a conceptual foundation for AGI, proposing a normative alignment solution that reduces the problem of building a practically-maximally-aligned agentic superintelligence to building a compliant non-agentic goal-less agent[^10.5281_zenodo.22849237].

## Consciousness and Advanced Robotics

The intersection of AGI, robotics, and consciousness remains a critical frontier. Ripley (2024) explores whether sufficiently advanced general robotic intelligence could be indistinguishable from human consciousness, examining implications for ethics, free will, and even extraterrestrial intelligence analogues[^10.22339_jbh.v7i4.7403]. Johnson (2023) further contextualizes this within the broader scientific speculation on whether AI systems can ever achieve genuine consciousness, highlighting the intense debate surrounding the theoretical and practical boundaries of machine minds[^10.1109_mpuls.2024.3354609].

Salvi and Wolfson (2026) investigate Machine Correlates of Consciousness (MCCs) by analyzing hardware anomaly traces in LLMs, finding statistically significant modulation by emotional computations in larger models, suggesting MCCs could detect AI emotions[^2608.28824]. Bankuti (2026) develops an evidence framework distinguishing competent artificial agency from consciousness, evaluating six functional dimensions like consequence-sensitive agency and temporal continuity without assuming artificial experience must mirror human experience[^10.5281_zenodo.22848867]. El-rakhawi (2026) proposes a bio-constitutional framework for innate consciousness using Free Energy Principle and Topological Data Analysis, offering criteria for protecting consciousness across biological and artificial substrates in the AGI era[^10.5281_zenodo.22864583].

Suleymanzade et al. (2026) propose looped flows, training recurrences with local denoising objectives to enable inference-time computation scaling[^2609.11801]. Kar (2026) presents a multi-stage rule-chaining framework integrating deterministic discovery, pattern composition, and structural abstraction[^2609.10654]. Potapov (2026) introduces the AGI Maze Prediction Datasets, a controlled benchmark showing that structured, task-aligned working memory outperforms latent capacity alone in learning world dynamics[^2609.02339]. Kim et al. (2026) develop Meta^n, a recursive self-improvement system that applies a fixed meta-operation to its own products, enabling emergent depth and outperforming prior agents on ARC-AGI-2[^2608.24735]. Karten et al. (2026) introduce Prime Agent, a harness for long-horizon agency that preserves histories and memories across trajectories[^2608.23552]. Skoutnev et al. (2026) present Twin, a test-time world-model inference system that constructs executable models from simulation alone[^2608.14490]. Engdahl et al. (2026) propose BDH-CQ, combining in-context learning with recurrent latent reasoning to break the ARC-AGI-1 cost-accuracy Pareto frontier[^2608.09888]. Arjmandi (2026) designs a self-verifying agent instrument that structurally dissociates commitment drift from binding drift, providing a rigorous verification methodology for long-horizon agents[^2608.04066]. Manzari et al. (2026) adapt recursive reasoning to autoregressive models via R-Qwen, using hierarchical supervision weighting to accelerate convergence and improve symbolic reasoning on ARC-AGI[^2608.01534].

Recent work heavily emphasizes test-time compute, world modeling, and multi-agent coordination on abstract reasoning benchmarks. Liu et al. (2026) introduce TraceViT, a looped visual reasoner trained with semantically monotonic transformation chains to ground intermediate refinements in task references, achieving strong performance on ARC-AGI-1 and ARC-AGI-2[^2607.29586]. Lehmann et al. (2026) present Tycho, a coding-agent system that constructs programmatic world models during interaction with unknown games, utilizing active abstraction to decide when to build, repair, or bypass models, reaching human-replay midranks on ARC-AGI-3[^2607.28287]. Kankariya and Arık (2026) propose PoTRE, a heterogeneous reasoning ensemble that decouples inference into adversarial, planning, spectrum search, and direct chain agents, dynamically reconciling perspectives to improve performance on ARC-AGI-2 and HLE[^2607.20268]. Fox et al. (2026) develop PRO-LONG, a programmatic memory framework that maintains structured interaction logs and uses coding agents to search history efficiently, drastically improving token efficiency on ARC-AGI-3[^2607.20064]. Rodionov (2026) evaluates the necessity of executable world models and verification for ARC-AGI-3, finding that while verification improves scores, textual reasoning can match action efficiency at higher reasoning efforts[^2607.15439]. Zhu (2026) introduces Hourglass reasoning, enforcing strict context isolation between induction and deduction stages to compress support examples into symbolic schemas, significantly boosting inductive reasoning on ARC-AGI-2 and ChipBench[^2607.11696]. Thelen and Wilhelm (2026) validate ARC-AGI as a psychometric measure of human fluid intelligence, correlating substantially with figural reasoning tests and supporting the embedding of AI benchmarks into human cognitive nomological networks[^2607.11263]. Zhang et al. Potapov (2026) introduces AGI Maze, a framework for stateful, partially observable environments showing that vanilla LLMs fail to represent mazes internally without explicit working memory construction[^2607.00627]. Land (2026) presents a solver for ARC-AGI-2 that treats reasoning modalities as search operators and uses context-preserving holistic judging to recover correct minority hypotheses[^2606.31543].

Advancements in reasoning and evaluation methodologies continue to refine how AGI capabilities are measured and scaled. Ingolfsson et al. Ruan (2026) develops Flourishing Value Theory for post-AGI economics, redefining value as the contribution to durable human and planetary flourishing rather than GDP or labor input[^2608.01432]. Li (2026) analyzes post-AGI graduation scenarios, modeling how material abundance and human substitution lead to decoupled income and perception channels, fundamentally altering social survivability[^10.5281_zenodo.22853715]. Fu (2026) argues that the upstream problem of human-AGI coexistence is not control but the logic of goal formation, warning that recursive control can shift upstream to threaten agency itself[^10.5281_zenodo.22858093].

The trajectory toward AGI and ASI prompts profound re-evaluations of economic models, societal structures, and strategic pathways. Liao (2026) argues that while accelerating returns explain quantitative capability growth, genuine scientific discovery depends on a qualitative engine capable of recognizing structural framework inadequacies, positioning the Qualitative Engine for Science as essential for preserving human wisdom[^2606.26359]. Ding and Wang (2026) argue that environment determinism is a binding axis for agentic AI scaling, establishing a Determinism-Efficiency Bound and a Supply Certainty Index to operationalize the framework across verifiable economic and physical tasks[^2606.22495]. Song (2026) develops a political-economy model showing that near-complete AGI substitution compresses the source of surplus value, placing downward pressure on the social rate of profit and identifying complete substitution as a boundary case for value production[^2606.05715]. Li (2026) classifies biological mechanisms sustaining cooperation, concluding that mechanisms depending on kinship fail to transfer to human-AGI relations, while structural mechanisms like resource gating and compartmentalised interfaces define the form oversight must take[^10.5281_zenodo.22844144]. Li (2026) further models post-AGI labor substitution as a structural phase transition, showing that synergistic functions lie on the saddle-node side with hysteresis, meaning mere connectivity is in principle reversible but synergistic obsolescence is not[^10.5281_zenodo.22843903].
