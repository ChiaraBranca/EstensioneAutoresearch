# Living Survey: Artificial_General_Intelligence

## Introduction

This document collects the scientific literature regarding **Artificial_General_Intelligence**.

## Theoretical Foundations and Architectures
Recent research has begun to formalize the mathematical and structural prerequisites for AGI. A notable contribution proposes a morphogenetic structure that replaces the traditional subjective unit, defining mental states through topological integrity and establishing the mathematical inevitability of AGI [^10.5281_zenodo.19034234]. Complementing this theoretical work, deep reinforcement learning is explored as a platform-independent framework for intelligent robot control, positioning it as a foundational mechanism for AGI applications [^10.48550_arxiv.2105.13806].

## Safety, Control, and Verification
Ensuring the controllability of AGI remains a critical research frontier. It is argued that provably satisfying human-specified requirements through formal verification and mechanistic interpretability constitutes the only viable path to controllable AGI [^10.48550_arxiv.2309.01933]. On the policy front, the integration of AGI into sensitive domains like healthcare requires rigorous conceptual discipline, as unquantifiable hazards and regulatory gaps pose significant risks [^10.1016_j.jrt.2026.100183].

## Consciousness and Philosophical Implications
The intersection of AGI and consciousness continues to generate intense theoretical debate. Investigations into advanced robotic intelligence question its potential indistinguishability from human consciousness and its implications for responsible AI deployment [^10.22339_jbh.v7i4.7403]. This speculation is further fueled by ongoing examinations of whether AI systems can ever achieve anything close to genuine consciousness [^10.1109_mpuls.2024.3354609]. Philosophically, these developments are closely tied to trans- and posthumanist movements, which explore the promises and perils of super-intelligence and cognitive enhancement [^10.1016_j.intell.2021.101563].

## Societal, Ethical, and Strategic Dimensions
The societal trajectory of AGI is heavily influenced by its underlying normative frameworks. Critical analyses link the drive for AGI to historical eugenics traditions, warning that unchecked development could centralize power and exacerbate systemic discrimination [^10.5210_fm.v29i4.13636]. Broader conceptual frameworks propose managing AI risks through optimized human-machine cooperation, outlining potential societal end-states that range from uncontrolled substitution to ethically driven automation [^10.3390_asi4020037]. Strategically, national initiatives are actively tracking progress toward general AI, with specific focus on machine learning, brain-inspired AI, and brain-computer interfaces [^10.51593_20210064].
