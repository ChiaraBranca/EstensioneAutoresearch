This document collects the scientific literature regarding **Artificial_General_Intelligence**.

## Recent Developments in AGI Research

Recent literature continues to explore the conceptual, architectural, and ethical dimensions of AGI. Policy discussions emphasize the need for conceptual discipline, particularly in high-stakes domains like healthcare, where the term AGI is often used ambiguously[^10.1016_j.jrt.2026.100183]. Concurrently, theoretical frameworks are proposing novel architectures for autonomous general intelligence, such as morphogenetic topologies that redefine the transition from computation to genuine autonomy[^10.5281_zenodo.19034234][^10.5281_zenodo.19034233]. 

Safety and controllability remain central concerns, with recent proposals advocating for provably safe systems as the only viable path to controllable AGI, leveraging formal verification and mechanistic interpretability[^10.48550_arxiv.2309.01933]. Ethical and sociopolitical critiques also highlight the risks of unregulated AGI development, linking certain normative frameworks to historical discriminatory practices and urging a shift toward defined, safety-testable tasks rather than undefined "all-knowing" systems[^10.5210_fm.v29i4.13636]. Furthermore, global research trajectories are being closely monitored, with specific national strategies explicitly targeting leadership in general AI capabilities by 2030[^10.51593_20210064].
