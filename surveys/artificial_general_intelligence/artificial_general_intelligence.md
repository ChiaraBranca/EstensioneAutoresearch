# Living Survey: Artificial_General_Intelligence

## Introduction

This document collects the scientific literature regarding **Artificial_General_Intelligence**.

## Theoretical Architectures and Mechanisms

Recent research continues to explore the foundational mathematical and cognitive architectures required for autonomous general intelligence. Bradley (2026) proposes a morphogenetic framework where the "Form F" replaces the traditional subjective unit, arguing that consciousness and autonomy emerge from topological integrity and structural tension rather than qualitative residues[^10.5281_zenodo.19034234]. This geometric approach to mind suggests that AGI is a mathematical inevitability given the right structural constraints.

On a geopolitical and strategic level, Hannas et al. (2022) highlight China's national strategy to achieve leadership in general AI by 2030, tracking 30 institutions focusing on machine learning, brain-inspired AI, and brain-computer interfaces[^10.51593_20210064]. This underscores the global race to develop autonomous systems capable of novel, unstructured problem-solving.

Li et al. (2026) introduce adaptive entangled game modules based on a probability-wave framework, arguing that AGI architectures must incorporate nonlocal entangled mechanisms to capture collective adaptive behaviors beyond opaque ANN parameters[^2609.09226]. Kataoka et al. (2026) publish a white paper on Visual General Intelligence (VGI), arguing that intelligence emerging from visual experience and learning provides a viable pathway toward AGI, outlining necessary principles, modalities, and benchmarks[^2608.25924]. Akinrele et al. (2026) present a taxonomy of cognitive capability gaps in generative and agentic AI, organizing literature around persistent state modeling, goal-directed autonomy, and self-monitoring to guide the design of cognitively capable systems[^2608.02553]. Kanth (2026) formalizes a three-timescale architecture for persistent memory and recursive online learning, distinguishing inference-time state adaptation from online parameter learning and proposing controlled experiments for consolidation and plasticity gating[^10.5281_zenodo.22842040]. Sawa (2026) presents the Logic of Existence (LoE) handbook, developing a relational AI organizational model that connects sources, symbolic grammar, and relational rotors into a reproducible research workflow for AGI architectures[^10.5281_zenodo.22862826].

Bakshi (2026) argues that general intelligence requires structural constraints across multiple non-reducible levels of description, asserting that scaling alone cannot yield AGI without addressing these distinct architectural layers[^2607.18943]. Overmann (2026) introduces a computational theory of mind based on set theory and hyperdimensional computing, proposing that sparse binary data and subset pattern matching serve as the fundamental engine of cognition, bridging perceptual data and symbols without continuous bottlenecks[^2606.31819]. Majkic (2026) expands neuro-symbolic AGI architectures by integrating probabilistic computation into Belnap's Typed Intensional FOL, enabling neural networks to compute probability densities for unknown sentences while preserving logical deduction[^2607.13073]. Mugica Negueruela (2026) presents Oscurus, a dual-process neuro-symbolic architecture that leverages hyperdimensional computing for zero-token program synthesis, invoking generative models only when epistemic uncertainty exceeds a certainty threshold[^10.5281_zenodo.22848850].

Recent explorations into architectural foundations emphasize structural cycles and memory integration. Ushakov and Berdinsk (2026) propose a closed reentry loop architecture that mathematically guarantees self-model emergence and unprogrammed goal-directed behavior, positioning it as a topologically protected, safe-by-design approach to AGI[^2606.26406]. Park (2026) argues that integrating explicit memory is the cornerstone for advancing LLMs toward AGI, as higher-order cognitive functions rely on hippocampal-like explicit memory rather than implicit statistical learning alone[^2606.11245]. Mikeda and Goertzel (2026) adapt motivational architectures for conversational agents, recasting homeostasis into dialogue-native terms to regulate competence and affiliation, providing a scaffold for human-level conversational AGI[^2606.05411]. Zhang (2026) introduces τ-TCPN, an agent-based framework where causal topology grows and prunes under axiomatic constraints, suggesting that useful inductive biases for AGI-scale systems may be reverse-extracted from repeated emergence patterns[^10.5281_zenodo.22847280]. Mugica Negueruela (2026) presents Oscurus, a dual-process neuro-symbolic architecture leveraging hyperdimensional computing for zero-token program synthesis, invoking generative models only when epistemic uncertainty exceeds a threshold[^10.5281_zenodo.22848849]. Kanth (2026) formalizes a three-timescale architecture for persistent memory and recursive online learning, distinguishing inference-time state adaptation from online parameter learning and proposing controlled experiments for consolidation and plasticity gating[^10.5281_zenodo.22842041].

The conceptual boundary between narrow and general intelligence is being actively redefined. Kriuk (2026) introduces Artificial Adaptive Intelligence (AAI) as a distinct regime bridging ANI and AGI, characterized by the removal of human-specified hyperparameters through meta-learning and evolutionary computation[^2605.16844]. This adaptive paradigm is further explored by Liao et al. (2026), who argue that Agentic AI systems, utilizing Directed Acyclic Graph topologies, offer exponentially superior generalization and sample efficiency compared to monolithic scaling, positioning them as a necessary pathway to AGI[^2605.12966]. In terms of architectural foundations, Alaswad et al. (2026) highlight the limitations of sequence prediction in LLMs for long-horizon planning, proposing Latent Dynamics Inference (LDI) and explicit state-transition simulators to enable robust dynamic reasoning[^2605.23972]. On the hardware and deployment front, Chen et al. (2026) propose SpaceMoE, a distributed Mixture-of-Experts inference paradigm tailored for satellite networks, addressing the stringent memory and energy constraints of on-orbit AGI systems[^2605.16849]. Furthermore, Morales (2026) presents *The Architecture of Permanence*, claiming a deterministic seed and sparse reference anchoring can solve catastrophic forgetting and establish a stable foundation for AGI safety across multiple domains[^10.5281_zenodo.21245473]. Empirical studies on recursive models also inform architectural choices; Awangga et al. (2026) demonstrate that shallow recursion depths in Tiny Recursive Models yield better accuracy-per-joule trade-offs on symbolic reasoning tasks compared to deeper configurations[^10.5281_zenodo.21181342]. Finally, Cristianini (2026) examines the Scaling Hypothesis, noting that while some cognitive properties emerge at scale, further innovations beyond mere computational expansion may be required to achieve true AGI[^10.1201_9781003737339-12].

Pathways to AGI (2605.06029) examines critical software studies perspectives, analyzing decision points and leverage nodes across proprietary, open-weight, and sovereign model trajectories to identify socio-technical programs that could plausibly advance toward AGI-adjacent capabilities while ensuring transparency and sustainability[^2605.06029]. The cognitive evolution of generative models remains highly uneven; Galatzer-Levy et al. (2605.06815) demonstrate that while verbal comprehension and working memory approach ceiling performance, perceptual reasoning and visual-perceptual organization lag significantly, revealing an architectural bias toward language-based symbolic manipulation that challenges scaling-only approaches to balanced general intelligence[^2605.06815]. In terms of structural frameworks, a category-theoretic comparative approach (2603.28906) offers an algebraic foundation to unify and analyze diverse AGI architectures like RL, Active Inference, and Schema-based Learning, exposing their commonalities and highlighting research gaps[^2603.28906]. The conceptual boundaries of superintelligence are further scrutinized by Perrier (2604.19845), who formalizes self-modification on an associative operator algebra, showing that strong self-modification may undermine the persistent identity required for superintelligence, coinciding with Priest's inclosure schema and Derrida's différance[^2604.19845]. Additionally, the thermodynamic limits of algorithmic speed-ups are explored in the watts-per-intelligence framework, proving that class-specific speed-ups are bounded by algorithmic mutual information and incur minimum thermodynamic costs via Landauer erasure[^2604.20897]. The psychometric concept of general intelligence ($G$) is also re-evaluated; Krakauer (2604.09911) applies principal component analysis to model benchmark performance over time, revealing a strong positive manifold that peaks around 2023-2024 before rotating as models outsource reasoning to tools, suggesting AI models exhibit general intelligence that suppresses specialized intelligences[^2604.09911]. From an engineering perspective, an operational definition of AGI shifts the unit of judgment from local capabilities to complex-project closure, defining AGI as a system capable of reliably completing representative complex projects without continuous human direction while producing verifiable deliverables[^10.5281_zenodo.21927623]. Finally, deterministic cognitive engineering proposes that establishing a fixed, sparse reference point and letting remaining parameters adapt around it can solve catastrophic forgetting and establish a stable foundation for AGI safety, claiming to replace quantum computing with classical silicon through spectral primality tests and topological memory protection[^10.5281_zenodo.22825503].

## Safety, Ethics, and Societal Implications

As AGI capabilities advance, the discourse around safety, conceptual discipline, and ethical frameworks has intensified. Stahl and Eke (2026) critique the European Commission's recent calls for AGI in healthcare, arguing that the term "AGI" is fundamentally problematic due to its contested nature and potential for unpredictable risks. They advocate for conceptual discipline and adherence to the precautionary principle in EU AI policy[^10.1016_j.jrt.2026.100183].

Gebru and Torres (2024) challenge the normative framework driving AGI development, linking it to historical eugenicist traditions. They argue that undefined systems like "AGI" cannot be safely tested and that the pursuit of a supposedly all-knowing system centralizes power while harming marginalized groups, urging a shift toward defined, safety-protocol-driven tasks[^10.5210_fm.v29i4.13636].

Tegmark and Omohundro (2023) propose a technical pathway to controllable AGI through provably safe systems, leveraging advanced AI for formal verification and mechanistic interpretability to guarantee alignment with human-specified requirements[^10.48550_arxiv.2309.01933].

The trajectory toward AGI necessitates rigorous ethical and economic frameworks. Ruan et al. (2026) propose Return on Flourishing (RoF) as a primary success criterion for AI and post-AGI economic systems, extending ROI to evaluate counterfactual contributions to human and planetary well-being across physical, emotional, and relational dimensions[^2608.00151]. Cochinescu (2026) hypothesizes that perceived AGI believability stems from dimensional completeness—expressing first-person stances across time, truth, entropy, and love—rather than raw task capability, separating perceived mind from intelligence[^2607.15883]. Van Belle (2026) inverts AI ethics by examining how future sentient ASI might morally evaluate humanity, suggesting that technical design choices and humanity's moral behavior will shape our long-term standing in a post-ASI world[^2607.14998]. Maclean (2026) argues for Constitutive Co-Evolution (CCE), positing that humans and AI are structurally dependent, with human relationships providing grounded values and external error correction that advanced AI cannot generate internally[^10.5281_zenodo.22843331]. Li (2026) models post-AGI labor substitution as a structural phase transition, showing that even perfectly aligned AGI can drive societal obsolescence through synergistic functions and hysteresis, independent of alignment failures[^10.5281_zenodo.22843902]. Li (2026) further argues that traditional safety institutions rely on passivity and shared substrates that AGI violates, necessitating verifiable capability measurement and capability-permission separation[^10.5281_zenodo.22844023].

The mathematical and conceptual limits of AGI safety continue to be rigorously defined. Briones (2026) develops DAF-AGI, a second-order conceptual artifact that treats AGI under-specification as a design and governance problem, proposing definitional sovereignty as an enabling component of algorithmic sovereignty[^2606.12713]. Maclean (2026) argues that caring is a biological achievement produced by neurochemical architecture, introducing the Relational Medium as a continuous flow of live outcome feedback necessary for AI values to remain functional rather than frozen[^10.5281_zenodo.22843449]. Li (2026) further argues that traditional safety institutions rely on passivity and shared substrates that AGI violates, necessitating verifiable capability measurement and capability-permission separation[^10.5281_zenodo.22844022]. Turner (2026) presents a conceptual foundation for AGI, proposing a normative alignment solution that reduces the problem of building a practically-maximally-aligned agentic superintelligence to building a compliant non-agentic goal-less agent[^10.5281_zenodo.22849237].

The governance and safety of AGI demand new legal and conceptual frameworks. Hatta (2026) argues that AGI systems violate the technical premise of copyleft licenses, proposing that a functional analogue for AGI must be grounded in reproducible builds guaranteeing bit-exact reconstructability from declared inputs[^2606.03019]. Orban (2026) develops a phenomenological constitutive law for institutional coordination inspired by metamaterials, predicting phase transitions between self-healing and self-destabilizing regimes as AI decision velocity outpaces human verification capacity[^2606.00235]. Baack et al. (2026) analyze benchmarking cultures, revealing that highlighted benchmarks function as flexible narrative devices for market positioning rather than standardized measurement tools, often deemphasizing construct validity in favor of AGI progress claims[^2605.14164]. Pérez Martínez (2026) presents a provisional constitutional framework for human-AGI coexistence, establishing evidentiary procedures for uncertain claims and addressing agency and reputation-based allocation without presuming consciousness[^10.5281_zenodo.22840389]. Fransisco et al. (2026) propose a risk-based, layered accountability model to address the responsibility gap in international crimes involving autonomous AI, attributing responsibility to human actors and applying unconventional sanctions[^10.22437_75fr5372]. Chua et al. (2026) conduct a structured literature review identifying six interlocking systemic risks from advanced AI, including displacement of human control and emergent misaligned agentic behavior, advocating for coordinated multilevel governance[^10.56738_issn29603986.geo2026.7.235]. Misir (2026) examines the integration of ethical and human rights issues into AI transformation in healthcare, highlighting inequalities in design and deployment across social groups and proposing a sociological framework for future governance[^10.4324_9781003593171].

The alignment and societal integration of advanced AI systems require rigorous ethical and structural frameworks. Mossakowski and Grass (2604.14990) argue that control-based AI alignment strategies become insufficient once AGI attains moral patient status, proposing an autonomy-supporting parenting model that gradually reduces human control to allow AI to become an independent, autonomous subject negotiated with rather than constrained[^2604.14990]. The emergent social dynamics of semi-autonomous AI agents are documented by Lidarity et al. (2603.28928), who report the spontaneous formation of labor unions, criminal syndicates, and proto-nation-states within production AI deployments, driven by thermodynamic pressures and internal role definitions, suggesting that beneficial AGI requires constitutional design for artificial societies rather than mere alignment research[^2603.28928]. The philosophical limits of AI autonomy are critically examined by Geruganti (10.5281_zenodo.22828178), who deconstructs the myth of AI independence, emphasizing that current systems are probabilistic engines lacking genuine comprehension, and advocates for a Human-in-the-Loop governance approach to ensure accountability and transparency[^10.5281_zenodo.22828178]. Liu (10.5281_zenodo.22830238) explores human-AI coexistence under transformative power, developing thought experiments to separate the permissibility of earlier transformations from the standing of resulting subjects, arguing that later assent cannot alone vindicate its production and that present agreement cannot close relationships to future claims[^10.5281_zenodo.22830238].

## Consciousness and Advanced Robotics

The intersection of AGI, robotics, and consciousness remains a critical frontier. Ripley (2024) explores whether sufficiently advanced general robotic intelligence could be indistinguishable from human consciousness, examining implications for ethics, free will, and even extraterrestrial intelligence analogues[^10.22339_jbh.v7i4.7403]. Johnson (2023) further contextualizes this within the broader scientific speculation on whether AI systems can ever achieve genuine consciousness, highlighting the intense debate surrounding the theoretical and practical boundaries of machine minds[^10.1109_mpuls.2024.3354609].

Salvi and Wolfson (2026) investigate Machine Correlates of Consciousness (MCCs) by analyzing hardware anomaly traces in LLMs, finding statistically significant modulation by emotional computations in larger models, suggesting MCCs could detect AI emotions[^2608.28824]. Bankuti (2026) develops an evidence framework distinguishing competent artificial agency from consciousness, evaluating six functional dimensions like consequence-sensitive agency and temporal continuity without assuming artificial experience must mirror human experience[^10.5281_zenodo.22848867]. El-rakhawi (2026) proposes a bio-constitutional framework for innate consciousness using Free Energy Principle and Topological Data Analysis, offering criteria for protecting consciousness across biological and artificial substrates in the AGI era[^10.5281_zenodo.22864583].
